Showing the current series in What's coming

Six ways (A to C first, D to F added after) to put the series name (Advent), its tagline and a short description in front of readers, inside or just above the Weekly devotional card. Each is shown on a phone (375px, the main design) and on desktop, in Dawn patrol and Night swell. The series data already has a title, tagline, both art images and the five-week outline; only the short description is new (a description line in series.yml). "Week 4 of 5" comes from the latest published devotional. Each new series would fill it in the same way.

A. Series ribbon on the card

Smallest change. A dark ribbon sits on top of the Weekly devotional card: "This season's series", the name, tagline, two lines of description and a five-segment progress bar.

B. Series art card

The Weekly devotional card gets the series art (the surfers in the dark lineup, already made for each theme) with ADVENT set over it. The text beside or below carries the description, "Week 4: Shepherds on the Night Shift" and the progress bar. Most visual, and each series brings its own picture.

C. Series banner above the card, with the weeks listed

A full-width dark banner between the section heading and the Weekly devotional card. ADVENT in big display type, the tagline, the description, and all five weeks listed with this week highlighted and the next weeks shown as coming. The loudest option, and it answers "what is this series about and where are we in it?" at a glance.

D. Callout inside the card

The series is context for the devotional, so it sits inside the Weekly devotional card as a callout with a left accent bar: label, name, tagline, description. The bar and the label (not colour alone) mark it as a different kind of text. Text is the ink colour on a faint tint, so contrast stays high in both themes. No new heights beyond about 120px, no images, no new interaction.

E. Series card with a week-by-week stepper

A stepper is the standard pattern for "where am I in a sequence". It is an ordered list. Each step has a number or a tick and a text label, never colour alone: done weeks show a tick and link to their devotional, this week has a "This week" tag and aria-current="step", coming weeks have a dashed marker and no link. One primary button, "Read this week's devotional". Vertical on a phone, five columns on desktop.

F. Series beside this week, a disclosure on phones

Progressive disclosure keeps the phone short. On desktop the series sits beside this week's devotional in two columns. On a phone it collapses to one 48px row, "Advent: Waiting for the light" with an About toggle (a real details element, keyboard and screen-reader friendly), and this week's devotional with its single button stays in view. The first phone shows it closed, the second open.

Comparison

  • A, ribbon: least change and least height (about 150px on a phone). Good if the section is already long. Modest pull.
  • B, art card: strongest feeling, reuses the series art that already exists for each theme. Taller (about 400px on a phone) and depends on the art cropping well.
  • C, banner with weeks: the most informative and the loudest. Tallest (about 440px on a phone). The week list could link each past week to its devotional.
  • D, callout: the smallest and calmest. A left-bar callout inside the card, about 120px. Easy to scan and nothing new to build or maintain, but it informs more than it grabs attention.
  • E, stepper: the standard pattern for progress through a sequence. Tick, number and text labels (not colour alone), past weeks link to their devotional, one primary button. The most useful for catching up; the heaviest block.
  • F, beside this week, with a disclosure: the series is one 48px row on a phone and opens on tap; side by side on desktop. Leads with this week's devotional and its button. The best balance of attention and length.
  • Standards applied to D, E and F: text at WCAG AA contrast in both themes (no faded or low-opacity text), every tap target at least 44px, a visible focus ring, meaning carried by text and shape as well as colour, real ol and details elements, one primary action per block, and no text laid over images.
  • All six need the same data: a description in series.yml (one or two sentences, about 100 characters), and the current week taken from the latest published devotional. They could also be mixed, for example A's ribbon with C's week list shown only on tap.
