- [x] **Context:** What comes before and after? What is the book's purpose?
  > Notes: John opens with the same words as Genesis 1:1 ("In the beginning"), so this is a new-creation story: the God who spoke the world into being now speaks His final Word in person. Matthew and Luke tell the birth from Bethlehem; John tells it from eternity. The prologue (1:1–18) ends, "No one has ever seen God; the only God, who is at the Father's side, he has made him known" (1:18). John writes "so that you may believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God, and that by believing you may have life in his name" (20:31). "Dwelt" in v14 echoes the tabernacle, where God's glory lived among Israel (Exodus 40:34); the Greek verb is related to the word for "tent" (check before relying on it, see Step 7).
- [x] **Meaning:** What did it mean to the original readers?
  > Notes: The Word, God's own self-expression, the Creator, has become a real human being in history. The light that the prophets promised (Isaiah 9:2, week 1) has come. Many rejected Him, but anyone who receives Him by faith is given a new birth and a place in God's family. The eyewitnesses ("we have seen his glory") saw God's glory in a human life, "full of grace and truth".
- [x] **Christ:** How does this passage point to Jesus and the gospel?
  > Notes: The whole passage is about Jesus: eternal God, Creator, life and light, the One who became flesh. The gospel is in v12: receiving Him and believing in His name gives "the right to become children of God", not by birth or effort ("not of blood nor of the will of the flesh nor of the will of man") but "of God" (v13). It also answers the question of Mark 4:41 ("Who then is this, that even the wind and the sea obey him?"): He is the Word through whom the wind and sea were made. That hands over to Wind & Sea next week.

Response: (1) read John 1:1–14 each day this week and, at v14, thank Jesus for coming all the way into your world, and receive Him (v12); (2) do for one person what He did for us: turn up. ### The Word Became Flesh

**Advent · 5 of 5**

#### Conditions

> *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God. He was in the beginning with God. All things were made through him, and without him was not any thing made that was made. In him was life, and the life was the light of men. The light shines in the darkness, and the darkness has not overcome it.*
>
> *There was a man sent from God, whose name was John. He came as a witness, to bear witness about the light, that all might believe through him. He was not the light, but came to bear witness about the light.*
>
> *The true light, which gives light to everyone, was coming into the world. He was in the world, and the world was made through him, yet the world did not know him. He came to his own, and his own people did not receive him. But to all who did receive him, who believed in his name, he gave the right to become children of God, who were born, not of blood nor of the will of the flesh nor of the will of man, but of God.*
>
> *And the Word became flesh and dwelt among us, and we have seen his glory, glory as of the only Son from the Father, full of grace and truth.*
>
> John 1:1–14 (ESV)

#### The Lineup

Boxing Day is the busiest day of the year at Sarah's surf school. Half the town got a foam board for Christmas, and the beach is full of beginners. She starts every lesson on the sand: how to lie on the board, how to jump to your feet. But the talk isn't what gets people up. It's the moment she wades into the cold water beside a nervous learner, holds the board steady and says, "I'm right here. I'll tell you when."

Shouting from the beach doesn't get anyone up. Getting in the water does.

#### The Word

Yesterday was Christmas. The wrapping paper is in the recycling and the leftovers are in the fridge. Today John helps us see who was really in that manger.

He doesn't start in Bethlehem. He starts before the beginning: "In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God" (John 1:1). A word is how you make yourself known, and Jesus is God making Himself known. "All things were made through him" (v3): every coastline, every wave, every person on the beach.

And He is light. "The light shines in the darkness, and the darkness has not overcome it" (v5). For four weeks of Advent we've waited in the dark for the light. Here it is.

Then comes the most surprising sentence ever written: "And the Word became flesh and dwelt among us" (v14). The God who made the sea didn't shout instructions from the shore. He got in the water. He became a real human being who got tired (John 4:6) and hungry, and wept at a friend's grave (John 11:35).

Not everyone wanted Him: "his own people did not receive him" (v11). "But to all who did receive him, who believed in his name, he gave the right to become children of God" (v12). Not a better mood for the holidays. A new family.

John was there. "We have seen his glory," he says, "full of grace and truth" (v14). He saw the same Jesus stand up in a boat and calm a storm, while His friends asked, "Who then is this, that even the wind and the sea obey him?" (Mark 4:41). John's answer: He's the Word who made them, and He came to be with us.

#### Paddle Out

Christmas is over, but He is still here. Use the quiet days this week to look again at who came. Read John 1:1–14 once a day, and each time you reach verse 14, stop and thank Jesus for coming all the way into your world. Then do what He did for one person who found Christmas hard: don't just send a message, turn up. A visit, a walk, a call.

#### Pray

Lord Jesus, You are the Word who was with God and is God. Everything was made through You, even the sea. Thank You that You didn't stay on the shore, but became flesh and lived among us, full of grace and truth. I receive You. Shine Your light into my dark places, and help me turn up for others the way You turned up for me. Amen.

#### Go Deeper (optional)
- **Talk it over with a friend:** Who showed you what God is like by turning up, not just by talking? Who could you do that for this week?
- **Read further:** John 1:15–18 (the One who makes God known), Hebrews 1:1–3 (God has spoken by His Son), then Mark 4:35–41, where Wind & Sea begins. Next week: Mark 4:35–41.
