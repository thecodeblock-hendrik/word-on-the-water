## The Lineup

Every break has one. Sixty-odd, grey in the beard, been surfing the same reef since before you were born. This week the forecast is the one everyone's been waiting for: long-period swell, light offshore all morning. The car park is buzzing.

He just shrugs. "I'll believe it when I see it."

You can't blame him. He's watched a hundred forecasts bust. Long waits do that to a man. After enough letdowns, you stop hoping, just to protect yourself.

## The Word

Zechariah knew about long waits. He was a priest, getting old, married to Elizabeth. Luke says they were both "righteous before God," and yet "they had no child" (Luke 1:6–7). In their world that was a public sorrow; Elizabeth calls it her "reproach" (v25). They had prayed for years. By now the answer looked like no.

Israel had been waiting even longer. Centuries had passed since God last spoke through a prophet.

Then, on the biggest day of Zechariah's working life, chosen to burn incense in the temple, the silence breaks. An angel stands by the altar: "Do not be afraid, Zechariah, for your prayer has been heard." A son is coming, who will "make ready for the Lord a people prepared" (v17).

And Zechariah answers like the old man in the car park: "How shall I know this? For I am an old man." He had hoped too long to risk hoping again.

So God silences him. Months without a word. It is discipline, but it is not cancellation. Listen to Gabriel: "my words, which will be fulfilled in their time" (v20). Zechariah's doubt didn't stop God's promise. It only stopped his mouth.

> Zechariah's doubt didn't stop God's promise. It only stopped his mouth.

When the silence ended, the first thing out of that mouth was praise (v64). He sang about "the sunrise" visiting "from on high to give light to those who sit in darkness" (v78–79). His son John would point to that light: Jesus.

God doesn't promise every prayer will be answered the way we want, or when we want. But He hears. He keeps His word in His time. And the light He promised has come.

## Paddle Out

Think of one prayer you've stopped praying because the wait went on so long that hoping started to hurt. Write it down.

Then pray it again every day this week, honestly: "Lord, I'd given up on this. Help me trust You with it." If you can, tell one mate what it is, so you're not carrying it alone.

## Pray

Father, there are prayers I stopped praying because I got tired of being let down. Forgive me for the times I've doubted You. Thank You that Your word doesn't depend on how strong my faith is. Thank You for Jesus, the sunrise from on high. Teach me to hope in You again. Amen.

## Go Deeper

**Talk it over with a mate:** Is there a prayer you've given up on? What would it look like to bring it back to God, even if you're not sure what He'll do?

**Read further:** All of Luke 1:5–25, then Luke 1:57–80 (the silence ends) and Malachi 4:5–6.
