## The Lineup

The alarm goes at 4:40. Suit still damp from yesterday, coffee in a flask, headlights on all the way to the point. You paddle out by feel. You can hear the sets before you see them, just the hiss of white water somewhere out in the black.

Then you sit. You can't make the sun come up any faster. All you can do is wait. But you're not wondering whether it will come. You've seen it a thousand times. You're just waiting for it.

Advent starts in the same place.

## The Word

Psalm 130 opens "Out of the depths." The man who wrote it is in the dark, and not only because life is hard. He knows his own sin: "If you, O LORD, should mark iniquities, O Lord, who could stand?" But he also knows this: "with you there is forgiveness."

So he waits. Not passively, and not hopelessly: "in his word I hope." Then he paints a picture. A watchman on the city wall, working the night shift, eyes on the horizon, counting the hours to first light. He can't hurry the dawn, but he knows it's coming. The psalmist says it twice: *more than watchmen for the morning.* That's how sure his waiting is, and how hungry.

> He can't hurry the dawn, but he knows it's coming.

God's people waited a long time. Isaiah promised that "the people who walked in darkness have seen a great light," and he even said where it would shine first: Galilee, "the way of the sea" (Isaiah 9:1). A few verses later he says who the light is: "to us a child is born," and "his name shall be called… Mighty God… Prince of Peace" (Isaiah 9:6).

Centuries later, Jesus made His home in a fishing town on the Sea of Galilee and began to preach. Matthew says that's when the light dawned (Matthew 4:13–16). The One who would "save his people from their sins" (Matthew 1:21) had come.

That's Advent. We remember the long wait for His first coming, and we wait now, just as sure, for His return. Whatever dark you're sitting in, the morning isn't a maybe. It has a name.

## Paddle Out

Each morning this week, before you switch on a light or pick up your phone, sit in the dark for two minutes. Name the darkness you're waiting in: a guilt you can't shift, a diagnosis, a debt, a prayer that hasn't been answered yet.

Then pray Psalm 130:5 over it, out loud: "I wait for the LORD, my soul waits, and in his word I hope." Then switch the light on.

## Pray

Lord, I'm sitting in the dark on some things, and I can't make the morning come. Thank You that with You there is forgiveness. Thank You for sending Jesus, the light that has already dawned. Teach me to wait for You like a watchman: awake, honest, and sure the sun is coming. Amen.

## Go Deeper

**Talk it over with a mate:** What darkness are you waiting in right now? What would it look like to wait with hope, instead of just gritting your teeth?

**Read further:** All of Psalm 130, Isaiah 9:1–7 and Matthew 4:12–17.
