# Christian Faith Knowledge Base — Questions & Answers (Draft for Review)

## Introduction

This knowledge base answers the questions people most often ask about the Christian faith, written from a broad evangelical position. It is a draft for review by a pastor or theologian before it powers the chatbot.

**Doctrinal basis.** The answers follow the shared core of the [GotQuestions.org Statement of Faith](https://bibleref.com/faith.html) and the [Bible.org Doctrinal Statement](https://bible.org/article/doctrinal-statement): the Bible is inspired and without error in the original writings and is the final authority; God is one God in three persons; Jesus is fully God and fully man, died for sins and rose bodily; salvation is by grace alone, through faith alone, in Christ alone. Bible.org asks for grace on non-essential beliefs, and this document does the same.

**Entry format.** Every entry has:

- **Also asked** – other wordings, to help the chatbot match questions
- **Short answer** – two or three sentences
- **Explanation** – the fuller answer
- **Key verses** – references so readers can check the answer against Scripture
- **Tags** – topics for search and grouping

**Contested topics.** Where Bible-believing evangelicals genuinely disagree (for example baptism, predestination, spiritual gifts, the age of the earth, end-times timelines), the entry is marked **Contested among evangelicals**. It summarises the main views fairly, takes no side, and points the reader to their local pastor.

**Originality and Scripture.** All answers are written fresh for this project; no text is copied from GotQuestions, Bible.org, Ligonier or other copyrighted sources. Bible references are given rather than long quotations, so any translation can be used; the public-domain World English Bible is the safest choice for quoting in the chatbot.

**Review note.** Each answer should be checked by a trusted pastor or theologian before use. Mark changes inline or leave comments on the entry.

## 1. God

### 1.1 Does God exist?

**Also asked:** Is there a God? · How do we know God is real? · Can you prove God exists?

**Short answer:** Christians believe God exists and has made himself known through creation, conscience, Scripture and, above all, Jesus Christ. God cannot be tested in a laboratory, but there are strong reasons to believe in him.

**Explanation:** The Bible does not try to prove God; it assumes him from its first verse. It teaches that creation itself points to a Creator, so that people are “without excuse” (Romans 1:20). Common arguments include the existence of the universe (everything that begins has a cause), its fine-tuning for life, the moral law we all sense, and the historical evidence for the resurrection of Jesus. Ultimately Christians believe God is known personally, not only argued for.

**Key verses:** Genesis 1:1; Psalm 19:1–4; Romans 1:19–20; Romans 2:14–15; Hebrews 11:6; John 1:18

**Tags:** God, existence, apologetics

### 1.2 Who is God?

**Also asked:** What is God like? · What do Christians believe about God?

**Short answer:** God is the one eternal, personal Creator of everything. He is holy, loving, just, all-powerful and all-knowing, and he exists as Father, Son and Holy Spirit.

**Explanation:** The Bible presents God as Spirit (John 4:24), not part of creation but its maker and sustainer. He is personal: he speaks, loves, grieves and acts. He reveals his character by his names (for example Yahweh, “I AM,” Exodus 3:14) and most fully in Jesus, who said, “He who has seen me has seen the Father” (John 14:9).

**Key verses:** Exodus 3:14; Exodus 34:6–7; Deuteronomy 6:4; Isaiah 40:28; John 4:24; 1 John 4:8

**Tags:** God, nature of God

### 1.3 What is the Trinity?

**Also asked:** How can God be three in one? · Is the Trinity in the Bible? · Do Christians worship three gods?

**Short answer:** The Trinity means there is one God who eternally exists as three distinct persons: the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit. Each is fully God, yet there are not three gods.

**Explanation:** The word “Trinity” is not in the Bible, but the teaching is. Scripture insists there is only one God (Deuteronomy 6:4), yet calls the Father God, the Son God (John 1:1; Titus 2:13) and the Holy Spirit God (Acts 5:3–4). The three are named together in baptism (Matthew 28:19) and blessing (2 Corinthians 13:14). Human analogies all fall short; the Trinity is a truth revealed by God that we accept even though we cannot fully understand it.

**Key verses:** Deuteronomy 6:4; Matthew 3:16–17; Matthew 28:19; John 1:1–3; Acts 5:3–4; 2 Corinthians 13:14

**Tags:** Trinity, God, doctrine

### 1.4 Is God a man or a woman?

**Also asked:** Why is God called “Father”? · Does God have a gender?

**Short answer:** God is Spirit and does not have a physical body or biological sex. The Bible uses masculine names such as Father and King because that is how God chose to reveal himself, while also using some motherly images of his care.

**Explanation:** Both men and women are made in God’s image (Genesis 1:27). Christians follow Scripture in calling God “Father” and using “he,” as Jesus taught (Matthew 6:9). Passages like Isaiah 66:13 compare God’s comfort to a mother’s, but they are comparisons, not names. The incarnate Son, Jesus, is truly male.

**Key verses:** Genesis 1:27; Isaiah 66:13; Matthew 6:9; John 4:24

**Tags:** God, Father, gender

### 1.5 Who created God?

**Also asked:** Where did God come from? · Has God always existed?

**Short answer:** No one created God. He is eternal, with no beginning and no end, and he is the uncaused cause of everything else.

**Explanation:** Only things that begin to exist need a cause. The Bible describes God as existing “from everlasting to everlasting” (Psalm 90:2). Asking who made God is like asking who made the first cause; by definition an eternal being was never made.

**Key verses:** Psalm 90:2; Isaiah 43:10; Isaiah 57:15; Revelation 1:8

**Tags:** God, eternity, apologetics

### 1.6 What are the attributes of God?

**Also asked:** What are God’s characteristics? · What does omnipotent mean?

**Short answer:** God’s attributes are the qualities that describe who he is. They include being eternal, unchanging, all-powerful (omnipotent), all-knowing (omniscient), everywhere present (omnipresent), holy, just, loving, merciful, faithful and good.

**Explanation:** Some attributes belong to God alone, like being eternal and self-existent. Others he shares in a limited way with people made in his image, like love, wisdom and justice. God’s attributes never conflict: his love is always holy and his justice is always good.

**Key verses:** Psalm 139:1–12; Psalm 147:5; Isaiah 6:3; Jeremiah 32:17; Malachi 3:6; 1 John 4:8

**Tags:** God, attributes, omnipotence

### 1.7 What does it mean that God is holy?

**Also asked:** What is God’s holiness?

**Short answer:** God’s holiness means he is completely set apart: separate from all sin and utterly pure and unique. It is why sin is so serious and why we need a Saviour.

**Explanation:** In Isaiah’s vision, angels cry “Holy, holy, holy” (Isaiah 6:3), the only attribute repeated three times this way. Isaiah’s reaction was to confess his own sin. God calls his people to be holy too (1 Peter 1:15–16), something made possible through Christ.

**Key verses:** Leviticus 11:44; Isaiah 6:1–7; Habakkuk 1:13; 1 Peter 1:15–16; Revelation 4:8

**Tags:** God, holiness

### 1.8 What does “God is love” mean?

**Also asked:** Does God love everyone? · How does God show his love?

**Short answer:** Love is not just something God does; it is part of who he is (1 John 4:8). God showed the greatest love by sending his Son to die for sinners.

**Explanation:** Within the Trinity, Father, Son and Spirit have loved each other eternally. God shows kindness to all people (Matthew 5:45), and he proved his love “in that while we were yet sinners, Christ died for us” (Romans 5:8). God’s love is not the same as approving of everything; it is a holy love that seeks our good.

**Key verses:** John 3:16; Romans 5:8; 1 John 4:8–10; Matthew 5:44–45

**Tags:** God, love

### 1.9 Is God both loving and just?

**Also asked:** How can a loving God punish people? · Is God angry?

**Short answer:** Yes. A good God must judge evil, and a loving God provides a way of rescue. At the cross God’s justice and love meet: sin is punished, and sinners are forgiven.

**Explanation:** God’s wrath is not a temper tantrum but his settled opposition to evil. Romans 3:26 says God is both “just and the justifier” of those who trust in Jesus, because Christ bore the penalty for sin. A judge who ignored evil would not be good.

**Key verses:** Exodus 34:6–7; Psalm 89:14; Romans 3:23–26; 2 Peter 3:9

**Tags:** God, justice, love, wrath

### 1.10 Is God in control of everything?

**Also asked:** What is God’s sovereignty? · Does God control what happens?

**Short answer:** Yes. God is sovereign, meaning he rules over all things and nothing happens outside his knowledge and purpose. He works even through evil and suffering for the good of those who love him.

**Explanation:** Scripture affirms both that God is sovereign and that people make real choices and are responsible for them (Genesis 50:20; Acts 2:23). How these fit together is debated in detail (see 3.17), but all evangelicals affirm God’s sovereignty and human responsibility.

**Key verses:** Genesis 50:20; Psalm 115:3; Proverbs 16:9; Isaiah 46:9–10; Romans 8:28; Ephesians 1:11

**Tags:** God, sovereignty, providence

### 1.11 Does God change his mind?

**Also asked:** Does God repent? · Can prayer change God’s mind?

**Short answer:** God’s character and purposes never change (Malachi 3:6). When the Bible says God “relented,” it describes his consistent response to people who change, such as when Nineveh repented.

**Explanation:** God declared he would judge sin and also that he would show mercy to those who turn from it (Jeremiah 18:7–8). When people repent, God’s mercy is not a change in who he is but a faithful response. Prayer matters because God has chosen to work through it.

**Key verses:** Numbers 23:19; Jeremiah 18:7–10; Jonah 3:10; Malachi 3:6; James 1:17

**Tags:** God, immutability, prayer

### 1.12 Is God the same in the Old and New Testaments?

**Also asked:** Why does God seem harsher in the Old Testament?

**Short answer:** Yes. The same God is revealed in both Testaments. The Old Testament shows much of his mercy and the New Testament shows his judgment; the difference is in how much God revealed, not in who he is.

**Explanation:** The Old Testament repeatedly calls God “merciful and gracious, slow to anger” (Exodus 34:6), and Jesus spoke about hell more than anyone. The cross displays both God’s judgment on sin and his love. Hebrews 13:8 says Jesus Christ is the same yesterday, today and forever.

**Key verses:** Exodus 34:6–7; Psalm 103:8–13; Malachi 3:6; Hebrews 1:1–2; Hebrews 13:8

**Tags:** God, Old Testament, character

### 1.13 Can we see God?

**Also asked:** Has anyone seen God? · What does God look like?

**Short answer:** God the Father is Spirit and invisible. He has made himself seen in Jesus Christ, and believers will one day see God face to face.

**Explanation:** John 1:18 says no one has ever seen God, but the Son has made him known. Old Testament appearances of God (like Moses’ encounter) were partial revelations of his glory. The promise for believers is that “they will see his face” (Revelation 22:4).

**Key verses:** Exodus 33:18–23; John 1:18; John 14:9; Colossians 1:15; Revelation 22:4

**Tags:** God, revelation, Jesus

### 1.14 What are the names of God in the Bible?

**Also asked:** What does Yahweh mean? · What is Jehovah?

**Short answer:** God’s personal name is Yahweh (often written LORD in English Bibles), meaning “I AM.” Other titles include Elohim (God), Adonai (Lord), El Shaddai (God Almighty) and Abba (Father).

**Explanation:** Each name highlights part of God’s character. “Jehovah” is an older English form of Yahweh. Many names are combined with descriptions, such as Yahweh Jireh, “the LORD will provide” (Genesis 22:14). Jesus applied the “I AM” name to himself (John 8:58).

**Key verses:** Genesis 17:1; Genesis 22:14; Exodus 3:13–15; John 8:58; Romans 8:15

**Tags:** God, names, Yahweh

### 1.15 Do Christians, Jews and Muslims worship the same God?

**Also asked:** Is Allah the same as the Christian God?

**Short answer:** All three faiths claim to worship the God of Abraham, but they describe him very differently. Christians believe God is truly known only through Jesus Christ, whom Judaism and Islam do not accept as God the Son.

**Explanation:** Christianity shares the Hebrew Scriptures with Judaism and honours the God of Israel. But the New Testament teaches that rejecting the Son means not having the Father (1 John 2:23). Islam explicitly denies the Trinity and the death and deity of Jesus. Christians can respect people of other faiths while holding that Jesus is the only way to the Father.

**Key verses:** John 5:23; John 14:6–9; Acts 4:12; 1 John 2:22–23

**Tags:** God, other religions, Islam, Judaism

### 1.16 How can I know God personally?

**Also asked:** How do I have a relationship with God?

**Short answer:** You can know God personally by turning from sin and trusting in Jesus Christ, who came to bring us to God. Then you grow in that relationship through prayer, the Bible and the church.

**Explanation:** Jesus defined eternal life as knowing the only true God and Jesus Christ whom he sent (John 17:3). Sin separates us from God, but Christ died “to bring us to God” (1 Peter 3:18). See Theme 3 for how to be saved.

**Key verses:** Jeremiah 29:13; John 17:3; 1 Peter 3:18; James 4:8

**Tags:** God, relationship, salvation

## 2. Jesus Christ

### 2.1 Who is Jesus?

**Also asked:** Who was Jesus Christ? · What do Christians believe about Jesus? · Who is my Saviour?

**Short answer:** Jesus is the eternal Son of God who became a man, lived a sinless life, died on the cross for our sins, rose from the dead and now reigns as Lord. He is the only Saviour and the centre of the Christian faith.

**Explanation:** Jesus was born in Bethlehem, grew up in Nazareth and taught in Israel in the first century. Christians believe he was far more than a teacher: he is “the Word” who was with God and was God, and who “became flesh” (John 1:1, 14). The title “Christ” means “Anointed One” (Messiah), the promised King of Israel.

**Key verses:** Matthew 16:15–16; John 1:1–14; John 20:31; Philippians 2:5–11; Colossians 1:15–20

**Tags:** Jesus, identity, Christology

### 2.2 Is Jesus God?

**Also asked:** Did Jesus ever claim to be God? · Is Jesus the Son of God?

**Short answer:** Yes. The Bible teaches that Jesus is fully God, the second person of the Trinity. He claimed divine authority and accepted worship, and the apostles called him God.

**Explanation:** Jesus forgave sins (Mark 2:5–7), claimed the divine name “I AM” (John 8:58), said “I and the Father are one” (John 10:30) and accepted Thomas’ words “My Lord and my God!” (John 20:28). His opponents understood these as claims to deity (John 10:33). “Son of God” in his case means sharing God’s very nature, not being created.

**Key verses:** John 1:1; John 8:58; John 10:30–33; John 20:28; Colossians 2:9; Titus 2:13; Hebrews 1:8

**Tags:** Jesus, deity, Trinity

### 2.3 Was Jesus fully human?

**Also asked:** How can Jesus be both God and man? · Did Jesus have human feelings?

**Short answer:** Yes. Jesus is fully God and fully man in one person. He had a real human body and mind, grew tired and hungry, wept and suffered, yet never sinned.

**Explanation:** The church has summarised this as one person with two natures, divine and human, without mixing or separating them (the Council of Chalcedon, AD 451). Because he is human, Jesus could represent us and die in our place; because he is God, his sacrifice has infinite worth.

**Key verses:** Luke 2:52; John 1:14; John 11:35; Philippians 2:6–8; Hebrews 2:14–17; Hebrews 4:15

**Tags:** Jesus, humanity, incarnation

### 2.4 Did Jesus really exist?

**Also asked:** Is there evidence Jesus was a real person? · Is Jesus a myth?

**Short answer:** Yes. Almost all historians, Christian and non-Christian, agree that Jesus of Nazareth was a real first-century Jewish teacher who was crucified under Pontius Pilate.

**Explanation:** Besides the New Testament, which contains early sources written within decades of his life, Jesus is mentioned by the Jewish historian Josephus (around AD 93) and the Roman historian Tacitus (around AD 116), who records his execution under Pilate. The rapid growth of the church in Jerusalem also needs a historical explanation.

**Key verses:** Luke 1:1–4; 1 Corinthians 15:3–8; 2 Peter 1:16; 1 John 1:1–3

**Tags:** Jesus, history, evidence, apologetics

### 2.5 What is the virgin birth and why does it matter?

**Also asked:** Was Mary a virgin? · Why was Jesus born of a virgin?

**Short answer:** The virgin birth means Jesus was conceived by the Holy Spirit in the womb of Mary without a human father. It shows that he is uniquely God’s Son and that salvation comes from God, not human effort.

**Explanation:** Matthew and Luke both record it, and Matthew links it to Isaiah 7:14. It marks the moment the eternal Son took on human nature (the incarnation). Evangelicals hold it as an essential teaching about who Jesus is.

**Key verses:** Isaiah 7:14; Matthew 1:18–25; Luke 1:26–38; Galatians 4:4

**Tags:** Jesus, virgin birth, incarnation, Christmas

### 2.6 Why did Jesus have to die?

**Also asked:** Why did Jesus die on the cross? · Couldn’t God just forgive us?

**Short answer:** Jesus died to pay the penalty for our sins so that we could be forgiven and reconciled to God. Because God is just, sin had to be dealt with; because God is loving, he dealt with it himself in his Son.

**Explanation:** The Bible says “the wages of sin is death” (Romans 6:23). Jesus, who had no sin, took our place as a substitute and bore our sin (Isaiah 53:5–6; 2 Corinthians 5:21). This is often called substitutionary atonement. His death also defeated the powers of evil and showed God’s love.

**Key verses:** Isaiah 53:4–6; Mark 10:45; Romans 3:23–26; Romans 5:8; 2 Corinthians 5:21; 1 Peter 2:24; 1 Peter 3:18

**Tags:** Jesus, cross, atonement, salvation

### 2.7 Did Jesus really rise from the dead?

**Also asked:** Is the resurrection true? · What is the evidence for the resurrection?

**Short answer:** Yes. Christians believe Jesus rose bodily from the grave on the third day. This is the foundation of the faith: without it, Paul says, our faith is useless.

**Explanation:** Key facts widely accepted by historians include: Jesus died by crucifixion; his tomb was found empty; many people, including sceptics like James and Paul, believed they saw him alive; and the disciples were transformed and willing to suffer for this claim. One of the earliest records, 1 Corinthians 15:3–8, lists eyewitnesses, including over 500 people at once.

**Key verses:** Matthew 28:1–10; Luke 24:36–43; John 20:24–29; Acts 2:24–32; 1 Corinthians 15:3–20

**Tags:** Jesus, resurrection, Easter, apologetics

### 2.8 Why is the resurrection important?

**Also asked:** What does the resurrection mean for me?

**Short answer:** The resurrection proves Jesus is who he claimed to be, shows God accepted his sacrifice, defeats death, and guarantees that believers will also be raised to eternal life.

**Explanation:** Romans 1:4 says Jesus was declared the Son of God with power by his resurrection. Romans 4:25 links it to our justification. Because he lives, believers have a living hope (1 Peter 1:3) and the promise of new bodies (1 Corinthians 15:20–23).

**Key verses:** Romans 1:4; Romans 4:25; 1 Corinthians 15:14–22; 1 Peter 1:3–4

**Tags:** Jesus, resurrection, hope

### 2.9 Where was Jesus between his death and resurrection?

**Also asked:** Did Jesus go to hell? · What does “he descended into hell” mean?

**Short answer:** Jesus told the repentant thief, “Today you will be with me in Paradise” (Luke 23:43). Beyond that, Christians interpret a few difficult passages differently.

**Contested among evangelicals.** Some understand 1 Peter 3:18–20 and Ephesians 4:8–10 to mean Christ proclaimed his victory in the realm of the dead; others read them differently. The phrase “he descended into hell” in the Apostles’ Creed is understood by many as “he descended to the grave” or to the realm of the dead (Hades). All agree Jesus did not suffer further for sin after saying “It is finished” (John 19:30). Speak with your local pastor for more.

**Key verses:** Luke 23:43; John 19:30; Ephesians 4:8–10; 1 Peter 3:18–20

**Tags:** Jesus, death, resurrection, contested

### 2.10 Why is Jesus called the Son of Man?

**Also asked:** What does Son of Man mean?

**Short answer:** “Son of Man” was Jesus’ favourite title for himself. It points to his true humanity and, especially, to the divine figure in Daniel 7 who receives an everlasting kingdom from God.

**Explanation:** In Daniel 7:13–14, “one like a son of man” comes on the clouds and is given dominion and worship. At his trial, Jesus applied this to himself (Mark 14:62), and the high priest called it blasphemy.

**Key verses:** Daniel 7:13–14; Mark 10:45; Mark 14:61–64

**Tags:** Jesus, titles, Son of Man

### 2.11 Is Jesus the Messiah promised in the Old Testament?

**Also asked:** What prophecies did Jesus fulfil?

**Short answer:** Yes. Christians believe Jesus fulfilled the Old Testament promises of a coming Saviour-King, including his descent from David, birth in Bethlehem, suffering and death for sin, and resurrection.

**Explanation:** Examples include Micah 5:2 (born in Bethlehem), Isaiah 53 (the suffering servant who bears sin), Psalm 22 (details of crucifixion) and Zechariah 9:9 (entering Jerusalem on a donkey). After his resurrection Jesus explained how all the Scriptures pointed to him (Luke 24:27, 44).

**Key verses:** Genesis 3:15; Isaiah 9:6–7; Isaiah 53; Micah 5:2; Luke 24:25–27, 44

**Tags:** Jesus, Messiah, prophecy

### 2.12 Was Jesus just a good teacher or prophet?

**Also asked:** Was Jesus only a moral teacher? · Do Muslims believe in Jesus?

**Short answer:** Jesus’ own claims rule out that option. He claimed to be God, to forgive sins and to be the only way to the Father. A mere good teacher would not say such things.

**Explanation:** C. S. Lewis famously argued that someone making Jesus’ claims is either deluded, deceiving, or truly Lord. Islam honours Jesus as a prophet but denies his deity and crucifixion, which Christians see as contradicting the eyewitness record.

**Key verses:** Mark 2:5–10; John 8:58; John 14:6; John 10:30–33

**Tags:** Jesus, identity, apologetics, other religions

### 2.13 Did Jesus ever sin?

**Also asked:** Was Jesus perfect? · Could Jesus have sinned?

**Short answer:** No. The Bible clearly teaches Jesus lived a sinless life, which qualified him to be the perfect sacrifice for others.

**Explanation:** He was tempted in every way as we are, “yet without sin” (Hebrews 4:15). His anger in the temple was righteous anger, not sin. Theologians debate whether Jesus could have sinned, but all agree he did not.

**Key verses:** 2 Corinthians 5:21; Hebrews 4:15; 1 Peter 2:22; 1 John 3:5

**Tags:** Jesus, sinlessness, temptation

### 2.14 What did Jesus teach?

**Also asked:** What was Jesus’ main message?

**Short answer:** Jesus’ central message was the coming of the kingdom of God and the call to repent and believe the good news. He taught love for God and neighbour, humility, forgiveness, and faith in himself as the way to eternal life.

**Explanation:** Key teaching includes the Sermon on the Mount (Matthew 5–7), many parables, and his summary of the law: love God with all your heart and love your neighbour as yourself (Matthew 22:37–40). He also taught plainly about his coming death and resurrection.

**Key verses:** Mark 1:14–15; Matthew 5–7; Matthew 22:37–40; John 3:16; John 14:6

**Tags:** Jesus, teaching, kingdom of God

### 2.15 Why did Jesus teach in parables?

**Also asked:** What is a parable?

**Short answer:** A parable is a short story from everyday life that teaches a spiritual truth. Jesus used them to reveal truth to those willing to listen while hiding it from those who had hardened their hearts.

**Explanation:** Jesus explained this himself in Matthew 13:10–17. Famous parables include the Prodigal Son, the Good Samaritan and the Sower. Each usually has one or a few main points rather than a hidden meaning in every detail.

**Key verses:** Matthew 13:10–17, 34–35; Luke 10:25–37; Luke 15:11–32

**Tags:** Jesus, parables, teaching

### 2.16 What miracles did Jesus perform, and why?

**Also asked:** Did Jesus really do miracles?

**Short answer:** Jesus healed the sick, cast out demons, calmed storms, fed thousands and raised the dead. His miracles showed his compassion and proved his authority as the Son of God.

**Explanation:** John calls them “signs” because they point to who Jesus is (John 20:30–31). Even his opponents did not deny that he did remarkable works; they attributed them to other sources (Mark 3:22).

**Key verses:** Matthew 11:4–5; Mark 4:35–41; John 2:11; John 11:38–44; John 20:30–31

**Tags:** Jesus, miracles

### 2.17 Where is Jesus now?

**Also asked:** What is Jesus doing now? · What is the ascension?

**Short answer:** Forty days after his resurrection, Jesus ascended to heaven, where he reigns at the right hand of God the Father and prays for his people. He is present with believers by his Spirit.

**Explanation:** The ascension is recorded in Luke 24 and Acts 1. Hebrews describes Jesus as our great high priest who “always lives to make intercession” for believers (Hebrews 7:25).

**Key verses:** Acts 1:9–11; Romans 8:34; Ephesians 1:20–22; Hebrews 7:25; Matthew 28:20

**Tags:** Jesus, ascension, intercession

### 2.18 Will Jesus come back?

**Also asked:** What is the second coming?

**Short answer:** Yes. Jesus promised to return personally, visibly and in glory to judge the living and the dead and to establish his kingdom fully. No one knows the date.

**Explanation:** The angels at the ascension said he would return “in the same way” (Acts 1:11). Christians differ on the order of end-time events (see Theme 12), but all evangelicals affirm the literal return of Christ.

**Key verses:** Matthew 24:30, 36; John 14:3; Acts 1:11; 1 Thessalonians 4:16–17; Revelation 22:20

**Tags:** Jesus, second coming, end times

### 2.19 What does it mean that Jesus is Lord?

**Also asked:** What does “Jesus is Lord” mean?

**Short answer:** Calling Jesus Lord means recognising him as God and as the rightful ruler of the world and of your life. It is one of the earliest Christian confessions.

**Explanation:** In the Greek Old Testament, “Lord” (kyrios) translates God’s name, so calling Jesus Lord identifies him with God. Romans 10:9 links confessing Jesus as Lord with salvation, and Philippians 2:11 says every tongue will one day confess it.

**Key verses:** Romans 10:9; 1 Corinthians 12:3; Philippians 2:9–11

**Tags:** Jesus, lordship, confession

## 3. The Gospel and Salvation

### 3.1 What is the gospel?

**Also asked:** What is the good news? · What is the message of Christianity?

**Short answer:** The gospel is the good news that Jesus Christ died for our sins, was buried and rose again, so that everyone who turns from sin and trusts in him is forgiven and receives eternal life as a free gift.

**Explanation:** Paul summarises it in 1 Corinthians 15:3–4. The gospel has four parts: God is holy and created us for himself; we have all sinned and are separated from him; Christ died and rose to rescue us; we receive this by faith, not by earning it. “Gospel” means “good news.”

**Key verses:** John 3:16; Romans 1:16; Romans 3:23–24; Romans 6:23; 1 Corinthians 15:1–4; Ephesians 2:8–9

**Tags:** gospel, salvation

### 3.2 How can I be saved?

**Also asked:** How do I become a Christian? · How do I get to heaven? · What must I do to be saved?

**Short answer:** You are saved by turning from your sin and putting your trust in Jesus Christ as your Saviour and Lord. Salvation is God’s free gift, received by faith, not earned by good works.

**Explanation:** When the jailer asked Paul “What must I do to be saved?” he answered, “Believe in the Lord Jesus” (Acts 16:30–31). Romans 10:9 says if you confess Jesus as Lord and believe God raised him from the dead, you will be saved. There is no special formula; you can pray in your own words, admitting your sin, thanking Jesus for dying for you, and asking him to be your Lord. Then tell another Christian and connect with a church.

**Key verses:** John 1:12; John 3:16–18; Acts 16:30–31; Romans 10:9–13; Ephesians 2:8–9

**Tags:** salvation, conversion, faith

### 3.3 What is sin?

**Also asked:** What counts as sin? · Is everyone a sinner?

**Short answer:** Sin is any failure to love and obey God, in thought, word, deed or attitude. It means missing the mark of God’s perfect standard. Everyone except Jesus has sinned.

**Explanation:** Sin includes wrong actions (lying, stealing), wrong desires (greed, lust) and failing to do good (James 4:17). At its root, sin is rebellion against God, putting ourselves in his place. Romans 3:23 says “all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God.”

**Key verses:** Romans 3:10–12, 23; James 4:17; 1 John 3:4; Matthew 5:21–28

**Tags:** sin, human nature

### 3.4 What is original sin?

**Also asked:** Are people born sinful? · Why are we punished for Adam’s sin?

**Short answer:** Original sin is the teaching that, because of Adam’s fall, every person is born with a sinful nature that inclines them away from God. We sin because we are sinners.

**Explanation:** Romans 5:12 says sin entered the world through one man and death through sin, spreading to all. David said he was sinful from birth (Psalm 51:5). The good news is that just as Adam brought sin, Christ, the “last Adam,” brings life (Romans 5:18–19).

**Key verses:** Genesis 3; Psalm 51:5; Romans 5:12–19; Ephesians 2:1–3

**Tags:** sin, original sin, the fall

### 3.5 What is grace?

**Also asked:** What does saved by grace mean? · What is the difference between grace and mercy?

**Short answer:** Grace is God’s undeserved kindness: giving us good we have not earned. Mercy is God not giving us the punishment we deserve. Salvation is entirely by grace.

**Explanation:** Ephesians 2:8–9 says we are saved by grace through faith, “not of works, that no one would boast.” Grace also keeps working after salvation, training us to live godly lives (Titus 2:11–12).

**Key verses:** Romans 3:24; Ephesians 2:4–9; Titus 2:11–14; Titus 3:4–7

**Tags:** grace, mercy, salvation

### 3.6 What is faith?

**Also asked:** What does it mean to believe in Jesus? · Is faith blind?

**Short answer:** Saving faith is trusting in Jesus Christ for forgiveness and eternal life. It is more than agreeing with facts; it is relying on him personally, like resting your weight on a chair.

**Explanation:** Biblical faith involves knowing the truth about Jesus, agreeing it is true and personally trusting him. It is not blind: it rests on God’s trustworthy word and the evidence of what he has done (Hebrews 11:1; John 20:31). Faith itself is a gift from God, not a work we boast in.

**Key verses:** John 3:16; John 20:31; Romans 10:17; Ephesians 2:8; Hebrews 11:1, 6

**Tags:** faith, belief

### 3.7 What is repentance?

**Also asked:** Do I need to repent to be saved? · What does it mean to repent?

**Short answer:** Repentance is a change of mind and heart about sin and God that leads to turning away from sin and turning to God. Repentance and faith go together as two sides of one coin.

**Explanation:** Jesus began his preaching with “Repent, and believe in the Good News” (Mark 1:15). Repentance is not earning forgiveness or becoming perfect first; it is a sincere turn. It continues through the Christian life whenever we sin.

**Key verses:** Mark 1:15; Luke 15:7; Acts 3:19; Acts 20:21; 2 Corinthians 7:10

**Tags:** repentance, salvation

### 3.8 What does “born again” mean?

**Also asked:** What is regeneration? · Do I have to be born again?

**Short answer:** Being born again means receiving new spiritual life from God through the Holy Spirit. Jesus said no one can see the kingdom of God unless they are born again.

**Explanation:** Jesus explained this to Nicodemus in John 3. It is not a special type of Christian; every true believer is born again. It is God’s work that makes a person a “new creation” with new desires to love and obey God (2 Corinthians 5:17).

**Key verses:** John 3:3–8; 2 Corinthians 5:17; Titus 3:5; 1 Peter 1:3, 23

**Tags:** born again, regeneration, Holy Spirit

### 3.9 What is justification?

**Also asked:** What does justified by faith mean?

**Short answer:** Justification is God’s act of declaring a sinner righteous in his sight because of Christ, not because of the person’s own goodness. It happens the moment we trust in Jesus.

**Explanation:** Our sin is credited to Christ, and his perfect righteousness is credited to us (2 Corinthians 5:21). This was a central truth of the Protestant Reformation: we are justified by faith alone (Romans 3:28; Galatians 2:16). Good works follow as the fruit of salvation, not its basis.

**Key verses:** Romans 3:21–28; Romans 4:4–5; Romans 5:1; 2 Corinthians 5:21; Galatians 2:16

**Tags:** justification, faith, righteousness

### 3.10 What is sanctification?

**Also asked:** How do Christians grow in holiness?

**Short answer:** Sanctification is the lifelong process in which the Holy Spirit makes a believer more like Christ. Justification is a one-time declaration; sanctification is ongoing growth.

**Explanation:** Believers are already set apart for God (1 Corinthians 6:11), are being transformed day by day (2 Corinthians 3:18) and will be made perfect when Christ returns (1 John 3:2). Growth happens as we cooperate with God through Scripture, prayer, obedience and the church.

**Key verses:** Romans 12:1–2; 2 Corinthians 3:18; Philippians 2:12–13; 1 Thessalonians 4:3; 1 John 3:2

**Tags:** sanctification, spiritual growth

### 3.11 Can I be saved by being a good person?

**Also asked:** Do good works get me into heaven? · Isn’t it enough to live a good life?

**Short answer:** No. No one is good enough to meet God’s perfect standard, and salvation cannot be earned. It is a gift received through faith in Christ. Good works are the result of salvation, not the way to get it.

**Explanation:** Isaiah 64:6 says our righteous acts are like filthy rags compared to God’s holiness. Ephesians 2:8–10 shows the order: saved by grace through faith, created for good works. James 2 teaches that real faith will produce good works.

**Key verses:** Isaiah 64:6; Romans 3:20; Galatians 2:16; Ephesians 2:8–10; Titus 3:5; James 2:17–18

**Tags:** salvation, good works, grace

### 3.12 Is Jesus the only way to God?

**Also asked:** Can people of other religions be saved? · Isn’t it narrow-minded to say Jesus is the only way?

**Short answer:** Yes. Jesus said, “I am the way, the truth, and the life. No one comes to the Father, except through me” (John 14:6). The apostles taught that salvation is found in no one else.

**Explanation:** This is Jesus’ own claim, not a later invention. If sin required the death of God’s Son, there can be no other way. The way is narrow, but the invitation is open to everyone, whatever their background (Revelation 7:9).

**Key verses:** John 3:18, 36; John 14:6; Acts 4:12; 1 Timothy 2:5

**Tags:** salvation, exclusivity, other religions

### 3.13 Can I be sure I am saved?

**Also asked:** How do I know I am a Christian? · What is assurance of salvation?

**Short answer:** Yes. God wants believers to know they have eternal life (1 John 5:13). Assurance rests on God’s promises in Christ, confirmed by the Spirit’s witness and a changed life.

**Explanation:** 1 John gives signs of genuine faith: believing in Jesus, obeying God, loving other believers and turning from sin. Doubts are common, and the answer is to look to Christ’s finished work, not our own performance.

**Key verses:** John 5:24; John 10:27–29; Romans 8:16; 1 John 5:11–13

**Tags:** assurance, salvation

### 3.14 Can a Christian lose their salvation?

**Also asked:** Once saved, always saved? · What is eternal security?

**Short answer:** Evangelicals agree that true believers are kept by God’s power and that genuine faith shows itself by perseverance. They differ on whether a true believer can finally fall away.

**Contested among evangelicals.** Many (especially Reformed and Baptist Christians) hold to eternal security or “the perseverance of the saints,” citing John 10:28–29 and Romans 8:38–39: those truly saved will never be lost, and those who fall away show they were never truly converted (1 John 2:19). Others (for example Wesleyan and some Arminian Christians) believe a person can turn away from the faith and be lost, citing Hebrews 6:4–6 and 10:26–29. Both views call believers to keep trusting Christ. Speak with your local pastor for more.

**Key verses:** John 6:37–40; John 10:27–29; Romans 8:38–39; Philippians 1:6; Hebrews 6:4–6; 1 John 2:19

**Tags:** assurance, eternal security, contested

### 3.15 What is the unforgivable sin?

**Also asked:** Have I committed the unpardonable sin? · What is blasphemy against the Holy Spirit?

**Short answer:** Jesus warned about blasphemy against the Holy Spirit (Mark 3:28–30), which in context was persistently rejecting the Spirit’s clear testimony about Jesus and calling his work evil. A person worried that they have committed it almost certainly has not.

**Explanation:** The Pharisees saw Jesus cast out demons by God’s Spirit and claimed he did it by Satan. The sin is unforgivable because it refuses the only source of forgiveness. Any sin that is repented of can be forgiven (1 John 1:9).

**Key verses:** Matthew 12:22–32; Mark 3:22–30; 1 John 1:9

**Tags:** sin, forgiveness, Holy Spirit

### 3.16 What is atonement?

**Also asked:** What does propitiation mean? · What did the cross achieve?

**Short answer:** Atonement is how God makes sinners “at one” with him. Through Christ’s death, sin is paid for, God’s just anger against sin is satisfied (propitiation), and believers are reconciled to God.

**Explanation:** The Old Testament sacrifices pointed forward to Christ, “the Lamb of God, who takes away the sin of the world” (John 1:29). The New Testament uses several pictures: substitution, ransom, reconciliation and victory over evil. Evangelicals see penal substitution (Christ bearing our penalty) as central.

**Key verses:** Leviticus 16; Isaiah 53:5–6; John 1:29; Romans 3:25; 2 Corinthians 5:18–21; 1 John 2:2

**Tags:** atonement, cross, propitiation

### 3.17 What is predestination? Do we have free will?

**Also asked:** What is election? · Calvinism vs Arminianism · Does God choose who is saved?

**Short answer:** The Bible teaches both that God chooses (predestines) people for salvation and that people are responsible to repent and believe. Evangelicals agree on both truths but differ on how they fit together.

**Contested among evangelicals.** Calvinists (Reformed) believe God chose specific individuals for salvation based on his grace alone, not on foreseen faith, and that his grace effectively brings them to faith (Ephesians 1:4–5; Romans 9). Arminians believe God’s choice is based on his foreknowledge of who would freely believe, and that people can resist his grace (1 Timothy 2:4; 2 Peter 3:9). Both affirm salvation is by grace through faith and that the gospel should be offered to everyone. Speak with your local pastor for more.

**Key verses:** John 6:37, 44; Acts 13:48; Romans 8:28–30; Romans 9; Ephesians 1:4–11; 1 Timothy 2:4; 2 Peter 3:9

**Tags:** predestination, election, free will, contested

### 3.18 What happens to babies and young children who die?

**Also asked:** Do babies go to heaven? · What about people who cannot understand the gospel?

**Short answer:** Many evangelicals believe God in his mercy saves those who die in infancy or who are unable to understand. Scripture does not speak directly, but it gives grounds for hope.

**Explanation:** When David’s infant son died, David said, “I will go to him” (2 Samuel 12:23). Jesus welcomed little children (Mark 10:14). God judges people for what they know and do, and young children have not yet consciously rejected him. Christians can trust the character of a just and loving God.

**Key verses:** Deuteronomy 1:39; 2 Samuel 12:22–23; Mark 10:13–16; Genesis 18:25

**Tags:** salvation, children, grief

### 3.19 What about people who never hear about Jesus?

**Also asked:** Is it fair that some never hear the gospel?

**Short answer:** The Bible teaches that everyone has some knowledge of God through creation and conscience and is accountable for it. Salvation comes only through Christ, which is why Christians are called to take the gospel to every nation.

**Explanation:** Romans 1–2 says all people know enough about God to be without excuse. God is perfectly just and will judge fairly (Genesis 18:25). Scripture gives no second way of salvation, so the church’s mission is urgent (Romans 10:14–15). Some evangelicals hold out hope that God may work in ways we do not know, but this is not taught clearly.

**Key verses:** Genesis 18:25; Acts 17:26–27; Romans 1:18–20; Romans 2:14–16; Romans 10:13–15

**Tags:** salvation, missions, justice

### 3.20 What does it mean to be adopted by God?

**Also asked:** Are all people children of God?

**Short answer:** Everyone is made by God, but the Bible says only those who receive Christ are adopted as God’s children, with full rights as members of his family and heirs of his promises.

**Explanation:** John 1:12 says those who receive Jesus are given the right to become children of God. The Spirit leads believers to call God “Abba, Father” (Romans 8:15). Adoption brings security, belonging and a future inheritance.

**Key verses:** John 1:12; Romans 8:14–17; Galatians 4:4–7; Ephesians 1:5; 1 John 3:1

**Tags:** adoption, identity, salvation

## 4. The Bible

### 4.1 What is the Bible?

**Also asked:** How many books are in the Bible? · Who wrote the Bible?

**Short answer:** The Bible is God’s written word: a collection of 66 books (39 in the Old Testament and 27 in the New) written by about 40 human authors over roughly 1,500 years, all telling one story that centres on Jesus Christ.

**Explanation:** The Old Testament was written mostly in Hebrew (with some Aramaic) and the New Testament in Greek. It includes history, law, poetry, prophecy, gospels and letters. Christians believe God guided each human author so that what they wrote is what God intended.

**Key verses:** Luke 24:27, 44; 2 Timothy 3:16–17; 2 Peter 1:20–21

**Tags:** Bible, canon, overview

### 4.2 What does it mean that the Bible is inspired?

**Also asked:** Is the Bible the word of God? · What is inspiration?

**Short answer:** Inspiration means the Bible is “God-breathed” (2 Timothy 3:16). God worked through human authors, using their personalities and styles, so that their words are also God’s words.

**Explanation:** Peter says the prophets “spoke from God, being moved by the Holy Spirit” (2 Peter 1:21). This did not usually mean dictation; Paul writes like Paul and John like John. Because Scripture comes from God, it carries his authority.

**Key verses:** 2 Timothy 3:16–17; 2 Peter 1:20–21; Hebrews 1:1–2

**Tags:** Bible, inspiration, authority

### 4.3 Is the Bible without error?

**Also asked:** What is inerrancy? · Is the Bible infallible?

**Short answer:** Evangelicals believe the Bible, in its original writings, is without error in everything it affirms (inerrant) and will never fail in its purpose (infallible), because it comes from a God who cannot lie.

**Explanation:** This applies to the original manuscripts, not to every copy or translation, though the text has been preserved with remarkable accuracy. Inerrancy allows for normal features of language such as round numbers, figures of speech and describing things as they appear (the sun “rising”).

**Key verses:** Numbers 23:19; Psalm 12:6; Psalm 119:160; John 17:17; Titus 1:2

**Tags:** Bible, inerrancy, authority

### 4.4 Can we trust the Bible we have today?

**Also asked:** Has the Bible been changed? · Is the Bible reliable? · Is the Bible true? · Is the Bible accurate?

**Short answer:** Yes. The Bible is the best-attested ancient document in existence. Thousands of manuscripts allow scholars to reconstruct the original text with very high confidence, and no core Christian teaching depends on an uncertain reading. The Bible also claims to be God’s truthful word (John 17:17), and the church has trusted it as such from the start.

**Explanation:** There are over 5,000 Greek manuscripts of the New Testament, some dating to within about 100–150 years of the originals, far more than for any other ancient work. The Dead Sea Scrolls, discovered from 1947, include a copy of Isaiah about 1,000 years older than the earliest one previously known, and it closely matches later copies.

**Key verses:** Isaiah 40:8; Matthew 24:35; John 17:17; 1 Peter 1:24–25

**Tags:** Bible, reliability, manuscripts, apologetics

### 4.5 Who decided which books belong in the Bible?

**Also asked:** What is the canon? · Why were some books left out?

**Short answer:** The church did not create the canon but recognised the books God had inspired. The tests included apostolic origin, agreement with accepted teaching and widespread use by the churches.

**Explanation:** The Old Testament canon was the Hebrew Scriptures Jesus used. By the end of the fourth century, church councils (such as Hippo in 393 and Carthage in 397) confirmed the 27 New Testament books already widely accepted. Later writings such as the “Gospel of Thomas” were rejected because they were late and taught different ideas.

**Key verses:** Luke 24:44; 2 Peter 3:15–16; Jude 3

**Tags:** Bible, canon

### 4.6 Why do Catholic Bibles have more books?

**Also asked:** What is the Apocrypha? · Should the Apocrypha be in the Bible?

**Short answer:** Catholic and Orthodox Bibles include extra books, called the Apocrypha or Deuterocanonical books, such as Tobit, Judith and 1–2 Maccabees. Protestants value some of them as history but do not regard them as inspired Scripture.

**Explanation:** These books were not part of the Hebrew Bible used by Jews and quoted by Jesus. Many early church leaders, including Jerome, distinguished them from Scripture. The Catholic Church formally declared them canonical at the Council of Trent (1546).

**Key verses:** Luke 24:44; Romans 3:1–2

**Tags:** Bible, canon, Apocrypha, Catholicism

### 4.7 Which Bible translation should I use?

**Also asked:** What is the best Bible translation? · Is the KJV the only true Bible?

**Short answer:** Many reliable translations exist. Word-for-word translations (ESV, NASB, KJV/NKJV) are close to the original wording; thought-for-thought translations (NIV, NLT) are easier to read. Choose one you will read and understand.

**Explanation:** Translations differ in style, not in core teaching. Many people use one for study and another for reading. Some Christians prefer the King James Version only, but most evangelical scholars hold that modern translations, based on older manuscripts, are equally faithful. The World English Bible (WEB) is a modern public-domain translation.

**Key verses:** Nehemiah 8:8; Psalm 119:105

**Tags:** Bible, translations

### 4.8 Does the Bible contradict itself?

**Also asked:** Are there mistakes in the Bible? · How do you explain Bible contradictions?

**Short answer:** Many apparent contradictions disappear when we read the context, recognise different perspectives, or understand ancient writing styles. Careful scholars have offered reasonable explanations for the commonly raised examples.

**Explanation:** Differences between the Gospels are like eyewitness accounts that emphasise different details, which actually supports their independence. Other examples involve copying errors in numbers in a few Old Testament passages, which do not affect any teaching. Difficulties call for humility and study, not for dismissing Scripture.

**Key verses:** Proverbs 30:5; Luke 1:1–4; 2 Timothy 2:15

**Tags:** Bible, contradictions, apologetics

### 4.9 How should I read and understand the Bible?

**Also asked:** How do I interpret the Bible? · Where should I start reading?

**Short answer:** Read prayerfully, in context and with attention to the type of writing. Ask what the passage meant to its first readers, what it teaches about God, and how it applies to you. Many people start with the Gospel of Mark or John.

**Explanation:** Good interpretation reads Scripture in its literary and historical setting, lets clearer passages explain harder ones and sees how each part points to Christ. A good study Bible and a church community help. The Holy Spirit gives understanding to those who seek it.

**Key verses:** Psalm 119:18; Luke 24:45; Acts 17:11; 2 Timothy 2:15; James 1:22

**Tags:** Bible, interpretation, Bible study

### 4.10 Should the Bible be read literally?

**Also asked:** Is the Bible meant to be taken literally?

**Short answer:** The Bible should be read according to its intended meaning. History is read as history, poetry as poetry, and parables and prophecy with attention to their symbols.

**Explanation:** Evangelicals take the Bible’s historical claims, such as the resurrection, as real events. At the same time, phrases like “God is my rock” are clearly figurative. Reading literally in this sense means reading as the author intended.

**Key verses:** Psalm 18:2; Luke 1:1–4; John 10:9; 2 Peter 1:16

**Tags:** Bible, interpretation, genre

### 4.11 Why should I read the Old Testament?

**Also asked:** Do Christians still follow the Old Testament?

**Short answer:** The Old Testament is God’s word and the foundation for the New. It tells of creation, the fall, God’s covenants and his promises of a coming Saviour, all of which are fulfilled in Jesus.

**Explanation:** Jesus said the Scriptures testify about him (John 5:39). Paul says they were written “for our learning” (Romans 15:4). Christians read the Old Testament through Christ: some laws, like sacrifices, are fulfilled in him (see 14.15).

**Key verses:** Luke 24:27; John 5:39; Romans 15:4; 1 Corinthians 10:11; 2 Timothy 3:15

**Tags:** Bible, Old Testament

### 4.12 Is the Bible enough, or do we need new revelation?

**Also asked:** What is the sufficiency of Scripture? · Does God still speak today?

**Short answer:** Evangelicals believe Scripture contains everything we need to know for salvation and for living a godly life. God may guide and speak to believers today, but any claimed message must be tested by Scripture and never adds to it.

**Explanation:** 2 Timothy 3:16–17 says Scripture equips us “for every good work.” Jude 3 speaks of the faith “once for all delivered to the saints.” Christians differ on how God guides personally today, but all agree the Bible is the final authority.

**Key verses:** Psalm 19:7–11; 2 Timothy 3:16–17; Jude 3; Revelation 22:18–19

**Tags:** Bible, sufficiency, revelation

### 4.13 Does archaeology support the Bible?

**Also asked:** Is there historical evidence for the Bible?

**Short answer:** Archaeology has confirmed many people, places and events described in the Bible, though it cannot prove its spiritual claims. Many details once doubted have since been supported.

**Explanation:** Examples include the Tel Dan inscription (9th century BC) mentioning the “House of David,” the Pool of Siloam and Pool of Bethesda in Jerusalem, and a stone inscription bearing the name of Pontius Pilate found at Caesarea in 1961. Archaeology often confirms Luke’s accuracy about local titles and places in Acts.

**Key verses:** Luke 1:1–4; Luke 3:1–2; John 5:2; John 9:7

**Tags:** Bible, archaeology, evidence, apologetics

### 4.14 Why are there four Gospels?

**Also asked:** What is the difference between Matthew, Mark, Luke and John?

**Short answer:** The four Gospels give four complementary portraits of Jesus, each written for a particular audience. Together they give a fuller picture, much like several witnesses describing the same events.

**Explanation:** Matthew shows Jesus as the promised Jewish Messiah-King; Mark presents him as the active servant and is the shortest; Luke, a careful historian, highlights his compassion for outsiders; John focuses on his divine identity so that readers may believe (John 20:31).

**Key verses:** Luke 1:1–4; John 20:30–31; John 21:25

**Tags:** Bible, Gospels, Jesus

### 4.15 Were the Gospels written by eyewitnesses?

**Also asked:** When were the Gospels written?

**Short answer:** The Gospels were written within the first century, within the lifetime of eyewitnesses. Matthew and John were apostles; Mark is traditionally linked to Peter’s preaching; Luke researched eyewitness accounts.

**Explanation:** Most scholars date Mark to around AD 55–70, Matthew and Luke a little later, and John around AD 90. Paul’s letters, written in the 50s, already summarise the core gospel. Early church writers such as Papias and Irenaeus link the Gospels to their traditional authors.

**Key verses:** Luke 1:1–4; John 19:35; John 21:24; 2 Peter 1:16; 1 John 1:1–3

**Tags:** Bible, Gospels, history, apologetics

## 5. The Holy Spirit

### 5.1 Who is the Holy Spirit?

**Also asked:** Is the Holy Spirit a force or a person? · Is the Holy Spirit God?

**Short answer:** The Holy Spirit is the third person of the Trinity, fully God, equal with the Father and the Son. He is a person, not an impersonal force: he teaches, guides, speaks and can be grieved.

**Explanation:** Jesus called the Spirit “another Helper” and used personal language for him (John 14:16–17, 26). Lying to the Holy Spirit is called lying to God (Acts 5:3–4). He can be grieved (Ephesians 4:30), which only a person can be.

**Key verses:** John 14:16–17, 26; John 16:7–15; Acts 5:3–4; 1 Corinthians 2:10–11; Ephesians 4:30

**Tags:** Holy Spirit, Trinity

### 5.2 What does the Holy Spirit do?

**Also asked:** What is the role of the Holy Spirit?

**Short answer:** The Holy Spirit convicts the world of sin, gives new birth, lives in every believer, helps them understand Scripture, guides, comforts, empowers them to live for God and gives gifts to serve the church.

**Explanation:** He was active in creation (Genesis 1:2), inspired Scripture (2 Peter 1:21) and raised Jesus from the dead (Romans 8:11). His central work is to glorify Christ and make him known (John 16:14).

**Key verses:** John 3:5–8; John 16:8–14; Romans 8:9–16, 26; Galatians 5:16–25

**Tags:** Holy Spirit, work of the Spirit

### 5.3 When does a person receive the Holy Spirit?

**Also asked:** Do all Christians have the Holy Spirit?

**Short answer:** Every true Christian receives the Holy Spirit at the moment they put their faith in Christ. The Spirit’s presence is the mark of belonging to Christ.

**Explanation:** Romans 8:9 says anyone who does not have the Spirit of Christ does not belong to him. Ephesians 1:13–14 says believers are “sealed” with the Spirit when they believe. Christians may experience fresh fillings of the Spirit over their lives (Ephesians 5:18).

**Key verses:** Acts 2:38; Romans 8:9; 1 Corinthians 12:13; Ephesians 1:13–14; Ephesians 5:18

**Tags:** Holy Spirit, conversion

### 5.4 What is the baptism of the Holy Spirit?

**Also asked:** Is Spirit baptism a second experience after salvation?

**Short answer:** Evangelicals agree that the Spirit unites believers to Christ and fills them for service. They differ on whether “baptism in the Spirit” happens at conversion or can be a distinct later experience.

**Contested among evangelicals.** Many evangelicals understand Spirit baptism as happening at conversion, when every believer is placed into the body of Christ (1 Corinthians 12:13), with ongoing fillings afterwards. Pentecostal and many charismatic Christians teach it is a distinct experience after conversion, often accompanied by speaking in tongues, pointing to Acts 2, 8, 10 and 19. Speak with your local pastor for more.

**Key verses:** Acts 1:5; Acts 2:1–4; Acts 8:14–17; Acts 19:1–6; 1 Corinthians 12:13

**Tags:** Holy Spirit, Spirit baptism, contested

### 5.5 What are spiritual gifts?

**Also asked:** What is my spiritual gift? · How do I find my spiritual gift?

**Short answer:** Spiritual gifts are abilities the Holy Spirit gives every believer to serve others and build up the church. Examples include teaching, serving, giving, leading, encouraging, showing mercy, faith and wisdom.

**Explanation:** Lists appear in Romans 12, 1 Corinthians 12 and Ephesians 4, and 1 Peter 4. Gifts are for the common good, not personal status. You often discover them by serving in your church and seeing where God bears fruit and others confirm it.

**Key verses:** Romans 12:4–8; 1 Corinthians 12:4–11; Ephesians 4:11–13; 1 Peter 4:10–11

**Tags:** Holy Spirit, spiritual gifts, service

### 5.6 Are miraculous gifts like tongues and prophecy for today?

**Also asked:** Have the gifts ceased? · Continuationism vs cessationism · Is speaking in tongues real?

**Short answer:** Evangelicals agree God can still do miracles in answer to prayer. They differ on whether gifts like tongues, prophecy and healing continue in the same way as in the early church.

**Contested among evangelicals.** Cessationists believe these sign gifts confirmed the apostles’ message and ceased once the New Testament was complete (citing Hebrews 2:3–4; Ephesians 2:20). Continuationists, including Pentecostals and charismatics, believe all the gifts remain available until Christ returns (1 Corinthians 13:8–12; 1 Corinthians 14:1). Both agree any gift must be used in love and order, and all prophecy must be tested by Scripture. Speak with your local pastor for more.

**Key verses:** Acts 2:4; 1 Corinthians 12–14; Ephesians 2:20; 1 Thessalonians 5:19–21; Hebrews 2:3–4

**Tags:** Holy Spirit, spiritual gifts, tongues, prophecy, contested

### 5.7 What is the fruit of the Spirit?

**Also asked:** What are the nine fruits of the Spirit?

**Short answer:** The fruit of the Spirit is the Christlike character the Holy Spirit grows in believers: love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness and self-control (Galatians 5:22–23).

**Explanation:** “Fruit” is singular: one character with nine qualities. Unlike gifts, which differ between Christians, every believer is meant to grow all of this fruit. It grows as we “walk by the Spirit” and stay connected to Christ (John 15:5).

**Key verses:** John 15:1–8; Galatians 5:16–25

**Tags:** Holy Spirit, fruit of the Spirit, character

### 5.8 What does it mean to be filled with the Spirit?

**Also asked:** How can I be filled with the Holy Spirit?

**Short answer:** Being filled with the Spirit means being continually led and empowered by him. It is a command for all Christians (Ephesians 5:18) and is shown in worship, thankfulness, obedience and boldness.

**Explanation:** In Acts, believers were filled repeatedly, often before speaking boldly about Jesus (Acts 4:31). Being filled involves confessing sin, surrendering to God, praying and living by his word.

**Key verses:** Luke 11:13; Acts 4:31; Ephesians 5:18–21; Galatians 5:16

**Tags:** Holy Spirit, filling, spiritual growth

### 5.9 Can I grieve or quench the Holy Spirit?

**Also asked:** What does it mean to grieve the Holy Spirit?

**Short answer:** Yes. Christians grieve the Spirit by sinful attitudes and actions, and quench him by resisting his leading. This does not remove the Spirit from a believer, but it hinders their walk with God.

**Explanation:** Ephesians 4:30 links grieving the Spirit to bitterness, anger and harmful speech. 1 Thessalonians 5:19 warns not to quench the Spirit. Confession restores fellowship (1 John 1:9).

**Key verses:** Ephesians 4:29–32; 1 Thessalonians 5:19–22; 1 John 1:9

**Tags:** Holy Spirit, sin, Christian living

### 5.10 How does the Holy Spirit guide me?

**Also asked:** How do I hear the Holy Spirit? · How do I know God’s will?

**Short answer:** The Holy Spirit guides mainly through Scripture, which he inspired, and also through prayer, wise counsel, circumstances and an inner conviction. True guidance never contradicts the Bible.

**Explanation:** Jesus promised the Spirit would guide his disciples into truth (John 16:13). Romans 8:14 says those led by the Spirit are God’s children. Most of God’s will is already revealed: to trust Christ, grow in holiness, love others and give thanks (1 Thessalonians 4:3; 5:18).

**Key verses:** Psalm 119:105; Proverbs 3:5–6; John 16:13; Romans 8:14; Romans 12:2

**Tags:** Holy Spirit, guidance, God’s will

### 5.11 What happened at Pentecost?

**Also asked:** What is Pentecost?

**Short answer:** Fifty days after Jesus’ resurrection, during the Jewish feast of Pentecost, the Holy Spirit came upon the disciples in Jerusalem as Jesus promised. They spoke in other languages, Peter preached, and about 3,000 people believed.

**Explanation:** Acts 2 describes the sound of wind and tongues of fire. Peter explained it as the fulfilment of Joel 2. Pentecost marks the birth of the church as a Spirit-filled community sent into the world.

**Key verses:** Joel 2:28–32; Acts 1:8; Acts 2:1–41

**Tags:** Holy Spirit, Pentecost, church history

## 6. Creation and Humanity

### 6.1 Did God create the world?

**Also asked:** Who made the universe? · What does the Bible say about creation?

**Short answer:** Yes. The Bible teaches that God created everything that exists, out of nothing, by his word. Creation reflects his power, wisdom and goodness.

**Explanation:** Genesis 1:1 opens: “In the beginning, God created the heavens and the earth.” The New Testament says all things were made through the Son (John 1:3; Colossians 1:16). Creation is good but not divine; we worship the Creator, not creation (Romans 1:25).

**Key verses:** Genesis 1–2; Psalm 33:6–9; John 1:1–3; Colossians 1:16–17; Hebrews 11:3

**Tags:** creation, God

### 6.2 How old is the earth?

**Also asked:** Were the days of creation literal 24-hour days? · Young earth vs old earth

**Short answer:** All evangelicals agree God created everything. They differ on how to read the days of Genesis 1 and therefore on the age of the earth.

**Contested among evangelicals.** Young-earth creationists read the days as six ordinary days and, using the genealogies, hold that the earth is thousands of years old. Old-earth creationists accept scientific dating of billions of years and read the days as long ages, or as a literary framework, while still affirming God’s direct creation. Both views are held by Bible-believing Christians who affirm inerrancy. Speak with your local pastor for more.

**Key verses:** Genesis 1:1–2:3; Exodus 20:11; Psalm 90:4; 2 Peter 3:8

**Tags:** creation, age of the earth, science, contested

### 6.3 Can a Christian believe in evolution?

**Also asked:** Does the Bible contradict evolution? · Did humans evolve?

**Short answer:** Evangelicals agree God is the Creator, that humans are uniquely made in his image, and that Adam and Eve were real people through whom sin entered the world. They differ on what role, if any, evolutionary processes played.

**Contested among evangelicals.** Many evangelicals reject evolution as an account of human origins, seeing it as incompatible with Genesis 1–2 and Romans 5. Some accept that God may have used evolutionary processes for other life while holding to the special creation of humans. A smaller group (evolutionary creationists) accept common descent while affirming God’s sovereign guidance. Christians should reject any view that removes God as Creator or denies the reality of the fall. Speak with your local pastor for more.

**Key verses:** Genesis 1:26–27; Genesis 2:7; Romans 5:12–19; 1 Corinthians 15:21–22

**Tags:** creation, evolution, science, contested

### 6.4 Were Adam and Eve real people?

**Also asked:** Is the story of Adam and Eve true?

**Short answer:** Evangelicals believe Adam and Eve were real, historical people, the first humans, created by God. Jesus and Paul both treated them as real.

**Explanation:** Jesus referred to the creation of male and female “from the beginning” (Matthew 19:4–5). Paul’s teaching that sin entered through one man and salvation comes through one man, Christ, depends on a real Adam (Romans 5:12–19). Luke traces Jesus’ genealogy back to Adam (Luke 3:38).

**Key verses:** Genesis 2–3; Matthew 19:4–5; Luke 3:38; Romans 5:12–19; 1 Timothy 2:13

**Tags:** creation, Adam and Eve, history

### 6.5 What does it mean to be made in the image of God?

**Also asked:** What is the imago Dei?

**Short answer:** Being made in God’s image means humans uniquely reflect God and represent him on earth. It is why every human life has equal dignity and worth.

**Explanation:** Genesis 1:26–28 links the image to ruling over and caring for creation as God’s representatives. It includes our capacity for relationship, reason, moral choice and creativity. The image was damaged by sin but not lost (Genesis 9:6; James 3:9) and is restored in Christ, the perfect image of God (Colossians 1:15).

**Key verses:** Genesis 1:26–28; Genesis 9:6; Psalm 8; Colossians 1:15; Colossians 3:10; James 3:9

**Tags:** humanity, image of God, dignity

### 6.6 What was the fall?

**Also asked:** What happened in the Garden of Eden? · Why did God put the tree there?

**Short answer:** The fall is when Adam and Eve disobeyed God by eating from the forbidden tree. Sin, death and brokenness entered the world, separating humanity from God.

**Explanation:** God gave Adam and Eve a good world and one command. Tempted by the serpent, they chose to trust their own judgment over God’s word (Genesis 3). The tree gave them a real choice to love and obey God. Immediately God promised a coming offspring who would crush the serpent (Genesis 3:15), the first hint of the gospel.

**Key verses:** Genesis 2:16–17; Genesis 3; Romans 5:12; Romans 8:20–22

**Tags:** the fall, sin, Genesis

### 6.7 Why did God create people?

**Also asked:** What is the meaning of life? · What is my purpose?

**Short answer:** God created people to know him, love him, enjoy him and reflect his glory, and to love one another and care for his world. Our deepest purpose is found in relationship with him.

**Explanation:** Isaiah 43:7 says God created people for his glory. The Westminster Shorter Catechism summarises that our chief purpose is “to glorify God, and to enjoy him forever.” God did not create because he was lonely; the Trinity has always had perfect love. He created to share his goodness.

**Key verses:** Genesis 1:26–28; Isaiah 43:7; Matthew 22:37–39; 1 Corinthians 10:31; Revelation 4:11

**Tags:** purpose, meaning of life, humanity

### 6.8 Do humans have a soul?

**Also asked:** What is the difference between soul and spirit? · What makes us human?

**Short answer:** Yes. The Bible teaches that humans are both physical and spiritual: we have a body and an immaterial soul or spirit that continues after death.

**Explanation:** Jesus said not to fear those who can kill the body but cannot kill the soul (Matthew 10:28). Christians differ on whether “soul” and “spirit” are two parts or two ways of describing one inner self, but they agree the whole person, body and soul, matters to God and will be raised.

**Key verses:** Genesis 2:7; Matthew 10:28; 2 Corinthians 5:6–8; 1 Thessalonians 5:23; Hebrews 4:12

**Tags:** humanity, soul, spirit

### 6.9 Why did God create male and female?

**Also asked:** What does the Bible say about gender?

**Short answer:** God created humans as male and female, equally in his image, with real differences that complement each other. Both are equally valuable and equally called to serve God.

**Explanation:** Genesis 1:27 presents both sexes as image-bearers. Genesis 2 shows the man and woman as partners in God’s work and the basis of marriage. Galatians 3:28 affirms equality in Christ. Evangelicals differ on how male and female roles apply in church and home (see 8.11).

**Key verses:** Genesis 1:27; Genesis 2:18–24; Matthew 19:4; Galatians 3:28

**Tags:** humanity, gender, male and female

### 6.10 Are people basically good?

**Also asked:** Are humans born good or bad?

**Short answer:** People are made in God’s image and capable of great good, but the Bible teaches that since the fall every person has a sinful nature. We all need God’s grace.

**Explanation:** Jeremiah 17:9 calls the heart deceitful; Romans 3:10–12 says no one is righteous. This does not mean people are as bad as they could be, but that sin affects every part of us. It also explains why humanity, with all its achievements, still produces injustice and cruelty.

**Key verses:** Genesis 6:5; Psalm 51:5; Jeremiah 17:9; Romans 3:10–23; Ephesians 2:1–3

**Tags:** humanity, sin, human nature

### 6.11 What does the Bible say about caring for creation?

**Also asked:** Should Christians care about the environment?

**Short answer:** Yes. God gave humans responsibility to rule over and care for creation as his stewards, not to exploit it. The earth belongs to God.

**Explanation:** Adam was placed in the garden “to cultivate and keep it” (Genesis 2:15). “The earth is the LORD’s” (Psalm 24:1). Christians look forward to the renewal of all creation (Romans 8:19–21), which gives our care for it lasting meaning.

**Key verses:** Genesis 1:28; Genesis 2:15; Psalm 24:1; Romans 8:19–22

**Tags:** creation, stewardship, environment

### 6.12 Was there death before the fall?

**Also asked:** Did animals die before Adam sinned?

**Short answer:** All evangelicals agree that human death entered through Adam’s sin. They differ on whether animal death existed before the fall, which is tied to the age-of-the-earth debate.

**Contested among evangelicals.** Young-earth creationists believe there was no animal death before the fall, since God called creation “very good” (Genesis 1:31) and Romans 8:20–22 links creation’s groaning to the curse. Old-earth creationists believe Romans 5:12 refers to human death, and animal death occurred over long ages before Adam. Speak with your local pastor for more.

**Key verses:** Genesis 1:31; Genesis 3:17–19; Romans 5:12; Romans 8:20–22

**Tags:** creation, death, the fall, contested

### 6.13 What does the Bible say about the value of human life?

**Also asked:** When does life begin? · What does the Bible say about abortion?

**Short answer:** Because every person bears God’s image, human life is sacred from conception to natural death. Evangelicals hold that unborn children are fully human and that taking innocent life is wrong.

**Explanation:** Psalm 139:13–16 describes God forming a person in the womb, and Luke 1:41–44 describes the unborn John leaping for joy. The command “You shall not murder” (Exodus 20:13) protects the vulnerable. The gospel also offers full forgiveness and healing to anyone affected by abortion.

**Key verses:** Genesis 9:6; Exodus 20:13; Psalm 139:13–16; Jeremiah 1:5; Luke 1:41–44

**Tags:** humanity, sanctity of life, abortion, ethics

## 7. Prayer and Spiritual Growth

### 7.1 What is prayer?

**Also asked:** Why should I pray? · Does God hear my prayers?

**Short answer:** Prayer is talking with God: praising him, thanking him, confessing sin and asking for what we and others need. Through Jesus, believers can come to God as a loving Father who hears them.

**Explanation:** Jesus prayed often and taught his followers to pray (Luke 11:1–13). Prayer is not about impressing God with words (Matthew 6:7) but about relationship and dependence. Because Jesus is our high priest, we can approach God’s throne with confidence (Hebrews 4:16).

**Key verses:** Matthew 6:5–13; Luke 11:1–13; Philippians 4:6–7; Hebrews 4:14–16; 1 John 5:14–15

**Tags:** prayer

### 7.2 How should I pray?

**Also asked:** Is there a right way to pray? · What should I say when I pray?

**Short answer:** Pray honestly, in your own words, trusting God through Jesus. The Lord’s Prayer is a model: praise God, seek his will, ask for daily needs, ask for forgiveness and protection.

**Explanation:** A helpful pattern is ACTS: Adoration, Confession, Thanksgiving and Supplication (asking). You can pray anywhere, any time, aloud or silently, standing, kneeling or walking. The Psalms show that honest prayer can include grief, questions and even complaint.

**Key verses:** Psalm 62:8; Matthew 6:9–13; Philippians 4:6; 1 Thessalonians 5:17

**Tags:** prayer, Lord’s Prayer

### 7.3 What is the Lord’s Prayer?

**Also asked:** Should we pray the Lord’s Prayer word for word?

**Short answer:** The Lord’s Prayer is the prayer Jesus taught his disciples in Matthew 6:9–13 and Luke 11:2–4. It can be prayed word for word and also serves as a pattern for all prayer.

**Explanation:** It begins by honouring God as Father and seeking his kingdom and will before asking for daily bread, forgiveness and deliverance from evil. Jesus warned against empty repetition (Matthew 6:7), so it should be prayed sincerely.

**Key verses:** Matthew 6:7–13; Luke 11:1–4

**Tags:** prayer, Lord’s Prayer

### 7.4 Why doesn’t God answer my prayers?

**Also asked:** Why does God say no? · Does God always answer prayer?

**Short answer:** God hears every prayer of his children, but he answers according to his wisdom: sometimes yes, sometimes no, sometimes wait. A loving Father does not give everything we ask for.

**Explanation:** Even Jesus prayed “not my will, but yours be done” (Luke 22:42), and Paul’s request to remove his “thorn” was answered with grace instead (2 Corinthians 12:7–10). The Bible also mentions things that hinder prayer, such as unconfessed sin or wrong motives (James 4:3; Psalm 66:18).

**Key verses:** Psalm 66:18–20; Isaiah 55:8–9; Luke 22:42; 2 Corinthians 12:7–10; James 4:3; 1 John 5:14

**Tags:** prayer, unanswered prayer, suffering

### 7.5 What does it mean to pray in Jesus’ name?

**Also asked:** Do I have to say “in Jesus’ name”?

**Short answer:** Praying in Jesus’ name means coming to God on the basis of who Jesus is and what he has done, and asking in line with his will. It is not a magic formula added at the end.

**Explanation:** Jesus is the only mediator between God and people (1 Timothy 2:5). His promise in John 14:13–14 is about prayers that fit his character and purposes. Ending with “in Jesus’ name” is a good habit that expresses this.

**Key verses:** John 14:13–14; John 16:23–24; 1 Timothy 2:5; 1 John 5:14

**Tags:** prayer, Jesus

### 7.6 Should Christians pray to Mary or the saints?

**Also asked:** Can the saints pray for us?

**Short answer:** Evangelicals pray to God alone, through Jesus Christ, the one mediator. The Bible gives no instruction or example of praying to people who have died.

**Explanation:** Believers may ask living Christians to pray for them (James 5:16). Mary is honoured as a model of faith (Luke 1:38, 46–55), but prayer and worship belong to God only. Scripture forbids attempts to communicate with the dead (Deuteronomy 18:10–12).

**Key verses:** Deuteronomy 18:10–12; Matthew 6:9; 1 Timothy 2:5; Hebrews 4:14–16

**Tags:** prayer, Mary, saints, Catholicism

### 7.7 What is fasting?

**Also asked:** Should Christians fast? · How do I fast?

**Short answer:** Fasting is going without food (or something else) for a time to focus on God in prayer. Jesus expected his followers to fast, but it is a voluntary practice, not a way to earn favour.

**Explanation:** Jesus said “when you fast,” not “if” (Matthew 6:16–18). In Scripture people fasted when seeking guidance, repenting or mourning (Acts 13:2–3; Joel 2:12). Anyone with health conditions should take medical advice before fasting from food.

**Key verses:** Isaiah 58:3–7; Matthew 6:16–18; Matthew 9:14–15; Acts 13:2–3

**Tags:** fasting, spiritual disciplines

### 7.8 How can I grow as a Christian?

**Also asked:** How do I get closer to God? · What are spiritual disciplines?

**Short answer:** Christians grow through the means God provides: reading and obeying the Bible, prayer, belonging to a church, worship, the Lord’s Supper, fellowship, serving and sharing their faith.

**Explanation:** The early church devoted itself to the apostles’ teaching, fellowship, breaking bread and prayer (Acts 2:42). Growth is the Spirit’s work, but we are called to make every effort (2 Peter 1:5–8). Consistency matters more than intensity.

**Key verses:** Acts 2:42; Philippians 2:12–13; Colossians 3:16; Hebrews 10:24–25; 2 Peter 1:5–8; 2 Peter 3:18

**Tags:** spiritual growth, discipleship, spiritual disciplines

### 7.9 Why should I read the Bible every day?

**Also asked:** How do I start a daily devotional? · Bible reading plan

**Short answer:** God’s word is spiritual food. Regular reading helps you know God, resist temptation, find guidance and grow in faith.

**Explanation:** Jesus answered Satan’s temptations by quoting Scripture: man shall not live by bread alone, but by every word from God (Matthew 4:4). Start small, such as one chapter a day from a Gospel, and ask: What does this say about God? About me? How should I respond?

**Key verses:** Joshua 1:8; Psalm 1:1–3; Psalm 119:9–11, 105; Matthew 4:4

**Tags:** Bible reading, devotions, spiritual growth

### 7.10 What is worship?

**Also asked:** Is worship just singing?

**Short answer:** Worship is giving God the honour he deserves. It includes singing and church gatherings, but it is really a whole life offered to God in love and obedience.

**Explanation:** Romans 12:1 calls offering our bodies as living sacrifices our “spiritual service” of worship. Jesus said true worshippers worship “in spirit and truth” (John 4:23–24). Corporate worship with other believers is also important (Hebrews 10:25).

**Key verses:** Psalm 95:1–7; John 4:23–24; Romans 12:1; Colossians 3:16–17; Hebrews 13:15

**Tags:** worship

### 7.11 What is meditation in the Bible?

**Also asked:** Should Christians meditate? · Is yoga or Eastern meditation okay?

**Short answer:** Biblical meditation means thinking deeply and prayerfully about God and his word. It fills the mind with truth, unlike forms of meditation that aim to empty the mind.

**Explanation:** Psalm 1 describes the blessed person who meditates on God’s law day and night. Christians differ on practices like yoga; the key question is whether a practice draws you toward Christ and his truth or toward another spiritual system.

**Key verses:** Joshua 1:8; Psalm 1:2; Psalm 119:15, 97; Philippians 4:8

**Tags:** meditation, spiritual disciplines

### 7.12 Why do I feel far from God?

**Also asked:** What if I don’t feel God’s presence? · Spiritual dryness

**Short answer:** Feelings rise and fall, but God’s presence with his children does not depend on how we feel. Seasons of dryness are common; keep praying, reading Scripture and staying connected to other believers.

**Explanation:** Many Psalms express feeling abandoned by God yet choose to trust him (Psalms 13, 42). Sometimes distance comes from unconfessed sin, sometimes from exhaustion or grief, and sometimes God is growing deeper faith. Jesus promised, “I am with you always” (Matthew 28:20). If low feelings persist, talk with a pastor and consider whether you need medical help.

**Key verses:** Psalm 13; Psalm 42; Isaiah 50:10; Matthew 28:20; Hebrews 13:5

**Tags:** spiritual dryness, doubt, prayer

### 7.13 Is it wrong to doubt?

**Also asked:** Can a Christian have doubts? · How do I deal with doubt?

**Short answer:** Doubt is not the same as unbelief. Many faithful people in Scripture struggled with questions. Bring your doubts honestly to God and look for answers in his word and from mature believers.

**Explanation:** A father cried to Jesus, “I believe. Help my unbelief!” and Jesus helped him (Mark 9:24). Thomas doubted the resurrection until Jesus met him (John 20:24–29). Jude 22 calls Christians to have mercy on those who doubt.

**Key verses:** Psalm 73; Mark 9:24; John 20:24–29; James 1:5–6; Jude 22

**Tags:** doubt, faith

## 8. The Church

### 8.1 What is the church?

**Also asked:** Is the church a building? · What is the body of Christ?

**Short answer:** The church is not a building but the people of God: everyone who belongs to Jesus Christ. It exists worldwide (the universal church) and in local congregations that meet together.

**Explanation:** The New Testament word for church means “assembly” or “gathering.” Paul calls the church the body of Christ, with Christ as head and each believer a member with a part to play (1 Corinthians 12:12–27). It is also called God’s family and a temple of the Holy Spirit.

**Key verses:** Matthew 16:18; Acts 2:42–47; 1 Corinthians 12:12–27; Ephesians 1:22–23; Ephesians 2:19–22; 1 Peter 2:9–10

**Tags:** church, body of Christ

### 8.2 Do I need to go to church to be a Christian?

**Also asked:** Can I be a Christian without going to church? · Why should I join a church?

**Short answer:** Going to church does not save you, but every Christian is called to belong to a local church. Faith is meant to be lived out together, not alone.

**Explanation:** Hebrews 10:24–25 urges believers not to give up meeting together. In a church, Christians are taught, cared for, held accountable and use their gifts to serve others. Online content can help, but it cannot replace a real community where people know you.

**Key verses:** Acts 2:42–47; Romans 12:4–5; Hebrews 10:24–25; Hebrews 13:17

**Tags:** church, membership, fellowship

### 8.3 What is the purpose of the church?

**Also asked:** What is the church’s mission?

**Short answer:** The church exists to worship God, build up believers to maturity in Christ, and make disciples of all nations through sharing the gospel and showing God’s love.

**Explanation:** Jesus gave the Great Commission: go and make disciples, baptising and teaching them (Matthew 28:18–20). Ephesians 4:11–16 describes leaders equipping believers so the whole body grows. The church also cares for the poor and needy as a sign of God’s kingdom (Galatians 6:10).

**Key verses:** Matthew 28:18–20; Acts 1:8; Ephesians 3:10; Ephesians 4:11–16; Galatians 6:10

**Tags:** church, mission, Great Commission

### 8.4 What is baptism?

**Also asked:** Why should I be baptised? · Is baptism necessary for salvation?

**Short answer:** Baptism is a command of Jesus and a public sign of being united with Christ in his death and resurrection. Evangelicals teach that baptism does not itself save; salvation is by grace through faith, but every believer should be baptised in obedience.

**Explanation:** Romans 6:3–4 pictures baptism as being buried with Christ and raised to new life. The thief on the cross was saved without baptism (Luke 23:43). Baptism is the first step of obedience and a declaration of faith before the church and the world.

**Key verses:** Matthew 28:19; Acts 2:38–41; Acts 8:36–38; Romans 6:3–4; Galatians 3:27; 1 Peter 3:21

**Tags:** baptism, church, sacraments

### 8.5 Should babies be baptised? How should baptism be done?

**Also asked:** Infant baptism vs believer’s baptism · Immersion or sprinkling? · Is infant baptism biblical? · Should babies be baptised or only adults who believe? · Do babies need to be baptised?

**Short answer:** Evangelicals agree that baptism is important and commanded. They differ on who should be baptised and how.

**Contested among evangelicals.** Baptists and many independent churches practise believer’s baptism, usually by immersion, for those who personally profess faith, noting that every clear New Testament example follows faith. Presbyterian, Anglican, Lutheran and Methodist churches also baptise the infants of believers, often by sprinkling or pouring, seeing baptism as a covenant sign like circumcision (Colossians 2:11–12) and noting household baptisms (Acts 16:15, 33). Speak with your local pastor for more.

**Key verses:** Acts 2:38–39; Acts 8:36–39; Acts 16:15, 31–34; Romans 6:3–4; Colossians 2:11–12

**Tags:** baptism, infant baptism, contested

### 8.6 What is communion (the Lord’s Supper)?

**Also asked:** What is the Eucharist? · Why do Christians take bread and wine?

**Short answer:** Communion is the meal Jesus gave his followers on the night before he died. Christians eat bread and drink the cup to remember his death, proclaim it until he returns and share fellowship with him and one another.

**Explanation:** Jesus said, “Do this in memory of me” (Luke 22:19). Paul warns believers to examine themselves and not take it in an unworthy manner (1 Corinthians 11:27–29). Evangelicals differ on how Christ is present in communion (as a memorial or as a real spiritual presence), but all reject the idea that it is a repeated sacrifice; Christ’s sacrifice was once for all (Hebrews 10:10–14).

**Key verses:** Matthew 26:26–29; Luke 22:19–20; 1 Corinthians 10:16–17; 1 Corinthians 11:23–29; Hebrews 10:10–14

**Tags:** communion, Lord’s Supper, sacraments

### 8.7 Why are there so many denominations?

**Also asked:** Which church is the right one? · Are denominations wrong?

**Short answer:** Denominations formed over history because of differences on secondary matters such as baptism, church government and worship style. Many share the same core gospel and recognise each other as fellow Christians.

**Explanation:** The most important question about a church is whether it faithfully teaches the Bible and the gospel of salvation by grace through faith in Christ. Christians can disagree on secondary issues while maintaining unity in essentials (Ephesians 4:3–6). Look for a church that preaches the Bible, honours Christ and loves people.

**Key verses:** John 17:20–23; 1 Corinthians 1:10–13; Ephesians 4:1–6; Romans 14:1

**Tags:** church, denominations, unity

### 8.8 How do I find a good church?

**Also asked:** What should I look for in a church?

**Short answer:** Look for a church that teaches the Bible faithfully, proclaims the gospel clearly, worships God sincerely, practises baptism and communion, has godly leaders and shows real love and care for people.

**Explanation:** Check its statement of faith, listen to the preaching and see whether it points to Christ and is grounded in Scripture. No church is perfect, so commit to one where you can grow and serve. Acts 2:42–47 is a good picture of a healthy church.

**Key verses:** Acts 2:42–47; 1 Timothy 3:1–13; 2 Timothy 4:2–4; Titus 1:9

**Tags:** church, choosing a church

### 8.9 How is the church led?

**Also asked:** What is an elder? · What is a deacon? · What does a pastor do?

**Short answer:** The New Testament describes elders (also called pastors or overseers), who teach and shepherd the church, and deacons, who lead in practical service. Leaders must show godly character.

**Explanation:** Qualifications are listed in 1 Timothy 3 and Titus 1, focusing on character more than gifts. Leaders are servants under Christ, the chief Shepherd (1 Peter 5:1–4). Churches differ in structure: congregational, elder-led, presbyterian or episcopal (bishop-led).

**Key verses:** Acts 6:1–6; Acts 20:28; 1 Timothy 3:1–13; Titus 1:5–9; 1 Peter 5:1–4

**Tags:** church, leadership, elders, deacons

### 8.10 Should Christians tithe?

**Also asked:** Do I have to give 10%? · What does the Bible say about giving?

**Short answer:** Christians are called to give generously, cheerfully and regularly to support the church and help those in need. Many use the Old Testament tithe (10%) as a helpful starting point, though the New Testament emphasises willing, proportionate generosity rather than a fixed rule.

**Explanation:** 2 Corinthians 9:7 says God loves a cheerful giver. Believers should give in proportion to what they have (1 Corinthians 16:2). Giving reflects trust in God and the generosity he showed us in Christ (2 Corinthians 8:9).

**Key verses:** Malachi 3:8–10; Luke 21:1–4; 1 Corinthians 16:2; 2 Corinthians 8:1–9; 2 Corinthians 9:6–8

**Tags:** giving, tithing, money, church

### 8.11 Can women be pastors?

**Also asked:** What roles can women have in church? · Complementarian vs egalitarian

**Short answer:** Evangelicals agree men and women are equal in value and both gifted to serve. They differ on whether the role of pastor or elder is limited to qualified men.

**Contested among evangelicals.** Complementarians believe the office of elder or senior pastor is reserved for men, citing 1 Timothy 2:12 and 3:1–7, while encouraging women in many other ministries. Egalitarians believe all roles are open to both men and women, citing Galatians 3:28, women leaders such as Phoebe, Priscilla and Junia (Romans 16), and seeing Paul’s restrictions as addressing specific local situations. Speak with your local pastor for more.

**Key verses:** Acts 18:26; Romans 16:1–7; 1 Corinthians 11:3–5; Galatians 3:28; 1 Timothy 2:11–15; 1 Timothy 3:1–7

**Tags:** church, women, leadership, contested

### 8.12 What is the difference between Catholics and Protestants?

**Also asked:** Are Catholics Christians? · What was the Reformation?

**Short answer:** The main differences concern authority and salvation. Protestants hold that Scripture alone is the final authority and that we are justified by faith alone; Catholic teaching places Scripture alongside church tradition and teaching authority and sees justification as a process involving faith, sacraments and works.

**Explanation:** The Reformation, beginning in 1517 with Martin Luther, sought to return the church to these biblical truths. Other differences include the role of the Pope, prayers to Mary and the saints, purgatory and the nature of the Mass. Evangelicals believe individual Catholics can be genuine believers through faith in Christ, while disagreeing with official teaching on these points.

**Key verses:** Romans 3:28; Galatians 2:16; Ephesians 2:8–9; 2 Timothy 3:16–17; 1 Timothy 2:5

**Tags:** church, Catholicism, Reformation, denominations

### 8.13 Why do Christians worship on Sunday?

**Also asked:** Should Christians keep the Sabbath on Saturday?

**Short answer:** The early church gathered on Sunday, “the first day of the week,” to celebrate Jesus’ resurrection, which happened on a Sunday. The Sabbath points to the rest we now find in Christ.

**Explanation:** Acts 20:7 and 1 Corinthians 16:2 show believers meeting on the first day. Colossians 2:16–17 says Sabbath days were a shadow of what was to come in Christ. Christians differ on how strictly to observe a day of rest, but all value weekly worship and rest as a gift from God (Romans 14:5–6).

**Key verses:** Exodus 20:8–11; Mark 2:27–28; Acts 20:7; Romans 14:5–6; Colossians 2:16–17; Hebrews 4:9–10

**Tags:** church, Sabbath, Sunday, worship

### 8.14 What is church discipline?

**Also asked:** Should a church correct its members?

**Short answer:** Church discipline is the loving process by which a church corrects members who persist in serious, unrepentant sin. Its aim is always restoration and the protection of the church.

**Explanation:** Jesus outlined the steps in Matthew 18:15–17: private conversation, then with witnesses, then the church. Paul urges gentleness and humility (Galatians 6:1). Discipline is never about shaming people but about calling them back to Christ.

**Key verses:** Matthew 18:15–20; 1 Corinthians 5; 2 Corinthians 2:5–11; Galatians 6:1

**Tags:** church, discipline, restoration

### 8.15 What are the Apostles’ and Nicene Creeds?

**Also asked:** Do evangelicals believe the creeds?

**Short answer:** The Apostles’ Creed and the Nicene Creed (AD 325, expanded in 381) are early summaries of core Christian beliefs about the Trinity, Christ and salvation. Evangelicals affirm their teaching because it agrees with Scripture.

**Explanation:** Creeds are not equal to Scripture, but they help the church state clearly what the Bible teaches and guard against error. The Nicene Creed, for example, affirmed that Jesus is “of one substance with the Father” against the Arian teaching that he was a created being.

**Key verses:** 1 Corinthians 15:3–4; 1 Timothy 3:16; Jude 3

**Tags:** church, creeds, church history

## 9. Suffering and Evil

### 9.1 Why does God allow evil and suffering?

**Also asked:** If God is good, why is there suffering? · What is the problem of evil?

**Short answer:** The Bible does not give a full explanation, but it shows that evil entered the world through rebellion against God, that God is not indifferent, and that he has acted in Jesus to defeat evil and will one day end all suffering.

**Explanation:** God created a good world and gave creatures real freedom; sin brought brokenness (Genesis 3; Romans 8:20–22). God can bring good out of evil, as he did with Joseph (Genesis 50:20) and supremely at the cross. Christians do not worship a distant God: Jesus himself suffered. Revelation 21:4 promises a future without tears, death or pain.

**Key verses:** Genesis 50:20; Job 38–42; Romans 8:18–28; 2 Corinthians 4:16–18; Revelation 21:3–5

**Tags:** suffering, problem of evil, apologetics

### 9.2 Is my suffering a punishment from God?

**Also asked:** Did God do this to me? · Am I being punished for my sins?

**Short answer:** Not necessarily. While sin can have consequences, the Bible warns against assuming that a person’s suffering is God’s punishment. For believers, Christ has already taken the punishment for sin.

**Explanation:** Job’s friends wrongly assumed his suffering was caused by sin. Jesus said a man’s blindness was not because of his or his parents’ sin (John 9:1–3). God may use hardship to train and refine his children (Hebrews 12:5–11), but this is the discipline of a loving Father, not condemnation (Romans 8:1).

**Key verses:** Job 42:7; Luke 13:1–5; John 9:1–3; Romans 8:1; Hebrews 12:5–11

**Tags:** suffering, punishment, comfort

### 9.3 How can I trust God when life hurts?

**Also asked:** Where is God when I suffer? · How do I cope with pain as a Christian?

**Short answer:** God promises to be near the broken-hearted and never to leave his people. You can be honest with him about your pain, lean on his promises and on other believers, and hold on to the hope of what is coming.

**Explanation:** The Psalms of lament show it is right to cry out to God (Psalm 13; Psalm 88). Jesus wept with grieving friends (John 11:35). Paul learned that God’s grace is sufficient in weakness (2 Corinthians 12:9). Seek support from your church, and from counsellors or doctors where needed.

**Key verses:** Psalm 34:18; Psalm 46:1; Isaiah 43:2; John 11:35; 2 Corinthians 1:3–5; 2 Corinthians 12:9; 1 Peter 5:7

**Tags:** suffering, comfort, trust

### 9.4 Why do bad things happen to good people?

**Also asked:** Why do the righteous suffer? · Why do the wicked prosper?

**Short answer:** We live in a fallen world where suffering affects everyone. The Bible acknowledges this puzzle openly and points to God’s final justice: one day every wrong will be put right.

**Explanation:** Psalm 73 struggles with why the wicked seem to prosper, until the writer considers their final end. Jesus said God sends rain on both the just and unjust (Matthew 5:45). Ultimately, the only truly good person, Jesus, suffered the most, for the sake of others.

**Key verses:** Job 1–2; Psalm 73; Ecclesiastes 9:11; Matthew 5:45; 1 Peter 4:12–19

**Tags:** suffering, justice

### 9.5 Does God cause natural disasters?

**Also asked:** Why does God allow earthquakes and floods? · Are disasters God’s judgment?

**Short answer:** The Bible teaches that creation itself is affected by the fall and “groans” as it waits for renewal. God is sovereign over nature, but we should not claim that any particular disaster is judgment on its victims.

**Explanation:** Jesus rejected the idea that people killed in a tower collapse were worse sinners than others, instead calling everyone to repent (Luke 13:4–5). Disasters remind us of the brokenness of the world and our need for God, and call Christians to help those affected.

**Key verses:** Luke 13:1–5; Romans 8:19–22; Psalm 46:1–3

**Tags:** suffering, natural disasters

### 9.6 Does God promise health and wealth to Christians?

**Also asked:** What is the prosperity gospel? · If I have enough faith, will God heal me?

**Short answer:** No. The Bible does not promise believers a life free of sickness, poverty or trouble in this world. The so-called prosperity gospel distorts Scripture; God’s greatest gift is himself, not material wealth.

**Explanation:** Jesus said, “In the world you have trouble; but cheer up! I have overcome the world” (John 16:33). Paul, a faithful apostle, suffered poverty, illness and persecution (2 Corinthians 11:23–30). God does sometimes heal and provide, and we should pray for this, but he also works through suffering.

**Key verses:** John 16:33; 2 Corinthians 12:7–10; Philippians 4:11–13; 1 Timothy 6:6–10; Hebrews 11:35–40

**Tags:** suffering, prosperity gospel, healing, false teaching

### 9.7 Does God still heal today?

**Also asked:** Should Christians pray for healing? · Should I see a doctor or just pray?

**Short answer:** Yes, God can and does heal, and Christians are encouraged to pray for the sick. He often heals through medicine and doctors too. He does not always heal in this life, but complete healing is promised in the resurrection.

**Explanation:** James 5:14–16 instructs the sick to call the elders to pray. Luke was a physician (Colossians 4:14), and Paul advised Timothy to use some wine for his stomach (1 Timothy 5:23). Using medical care is not a lack of faith.

**Key verses:** Psalm 103:2–3; James 5:14–16; 2 Corinthians 12:7–9; Revelation 21:4

**Tags:** healing, prayer, suffering

### 9.8 What does the book of Job teach about suffering?

**Also asked:** Why did God allow Job to suffer?

**Short answer:** Job shows that suffering is not always caused by personal sin, that God allows faith to be tested, and that God’s wisdom is far beyond ours. Job never received a full explanation, but he met God and was restored.

**Explanation:** Job’s friends gave wrong answers by blaming him. God’s speeches (Job 38–41) do not explain the reasons but reveal God’s greatness and care over creation. James 5:11 points to Job as an example of endurance and of the Lord’s compassion.

**Key verses:** Job 1–2; Job 38–42; James 5:11

**Tags:** suffering, Job

### 9.9 Why are Christians persecuted?

**Also asked:** Should Christians expect persecution?

**Short answer:** Jesus warned his followers that the world would oppose them because it opposed him. Persecution is not a sign God has abandoned them; he promises to be with them and reward their faithfulness.

**Explanation:** Millions of Christians today face discrimination or violence for their faith. Believers are called to pray for persecuted Christians, to love their enemies and to respond with courage and grace (Matthew 5:10–12, 44).

**Key verses:** Matthew 5:10–12, 44; John 15:18–20; 2 Timothy 3:12; Hebrews 13:3; 1 Peter 4:12–16

**Tags:** persecution, suffering

### 9.10 How should I grieve as a Christian?

**Also asked:** Is it wrong for Christians to grieve? · How do I cope with losing a loved one?

**Short answer:** Grief is a natural and right response to loss. Christians grieve, but not “as those who have no hope,” because of Jesus’ resurrection and the promise of reunion for those in Christ.

**Explanation:** Jesus wept at Lazarus’ tomb even though he was about to raise him (John 11:35). Grief can last a long time and come in waves. Share your sorrow with God and others; churches, grief counsellors and support groups can help.

**Key verses:** Psalm 34:18; Ecclesiastes 3:4; John 11:25–35; 1 Thessalonians 4:13–18; Revelation 21:4

**Tags:** grief, death, comfort

### 9.11 What does the Bible say about depression and anxiety?

**Also asked:** Can Christians be depressed? · Is anxiety a sin?

**Short answer:** Christians can and do experience depression and anxiety. Scripture shows faithful people like David and Elijah in deep despair, and God met them with care. Prayer, Scripture, community and, where needed, professional and medical help all have a place.

**Explanation:** Elijah wished to die, and God first gave him rest and food before speaking to him (1 Kings 19:4–8). Philippians 4:6–7 invites us to bring anxieties to God in prayer. Struggling is not a lack of faith. If you are in crisis or thinking of harming yourself, please reach out right away: in South Africa, call the SADAG Suicide Crisis Helpline on 0800 567 567 (free, 24 hours) or the LifeLine South Africa National Counselling Line on 0861 322 322 (24 hours). If your life is in immediate danger, contact emergency services.

**Key verses:** 1 Kings 19:1–8; Psalm 42:5; Matthew 11:28–30; Philippians 4:6–7; 1 Peter 5:7

**Tags:** mental health, depression, anxiety, comfort

### 9.12 Will God ever end suffering?

**Also asked:** Will there be suffering in heaven?

**Short answer:** Yes. The Bible promises that when Christ returns, God will make all things new. There will be no more death, mourning, crying or pain.

**Explanation:** Revelation 21:1–5 describes a new heaven and new earth where God dwells with his people. Paul said present sufferings are not worth comparing with the glory to come (Romans 8:18). This hope sustains Christians through hardship.

**Key verses:** Isaiah 25:8; Romans 8:18–25; 2 Corinthians 4:17; Revelation 21:1–5

**Tags:** suffering, hope, new creation

## 10. Death and the Afterlife

### 10.1 What happens when we die?

**Also asked:** Where do we go after death? · Is there life after death?

**Short answer:** At death the body dies but the person continues to exist. Believers go immediately to be with Christ; those without Christ are separated from God. At Christ’s return, all will be raised bodily for final judgment.

**Explanation:** Paul said to be “absent from the body” is to be “at home with the Lord” (2 Corinthians 5:8), and that dying is “far better” because he would be with Christ (Philippians 1:23). The intermediate state is not the final state: the hope is resurrection and a renewed creation.

**Key verses:** Luke 16:19–31; Luke 23:43; 2 Corinthians 5:6–8; Philippians 1:21–23; Hebrews 9:27

**Tags:** death, afterlife, intermediate state

### 10.2 What is heaven like?

**Also asked:** What will we do in heaven? · Is heaven boring?

**Short answer:** Heaven is above all being with God. The final hope is a new heaven and new earth where God lives with his people, with no sin, death or sorrow, filled with joy, worship, meaningful work and fellowship.

**Explanation:** Revelation 21–22 describes the new creation with rich imagery: a city, a river, the tree of life and the light of God’s glory. Believers will have resurrected bodies like Christ’s (Philippians 3:21). It will be far from boring; it is life as it was always meant to be.

**Key verses:** Isaiah 65:17–25; John 14:1–3; 1 Corinthians 2:9; Philippians 3:20–21; Revelation 21–22

**Tags:** heaven, new creation, afterlife

### 10.3 Is hell real?

**Also asked:** What is hell like? · Does the Bible teach eternal punishment?

**Short answer:** Yes. Jesus taught clearly about hell as a real place of judgment and separation from God’s blessing for those who reject him. It is described with images of fire and outer darkness.

**Explanation:** Jesus spoke of “eternal punishment” alongside “eternal life” (Matthew 25:46). Hell is just because sin against an infinitely holy God is serious. God takes no pleasure in the death of the wicked (Ezekiel 33:11) and has provided a way of escape in Christ. Most evangelicals hold that hell is eternal conscious punishment; a minority hold that the lost are ultimately destroyed (annihilationism).

**Key verses:** Matthew 10:28; Matthew 25:41–46; Mark 9:43–48; 2 Thessalonians 1:7–9; Revelation 20:11–15

**Tags:** hell, judgment, afterlife

### 10.4 How can a loving God send people to hell?

**Also asked:** Isn’t eternal punishment unfair?

**Short answer:** God does not delight in judgment; he went to the cross to save people from it. Those in hell receive the just result of rejecting God, who is the source of all good. In a sense, God gives them what they chose.

**Explanation:** God is both loving and just (see 1.9). A God who ignored evil would not be good. 2 Peter 3:9 says God is patient, not wanting anyone to perish but all to come to repentance. The invitation of the gospel is open to everyone.

**Key verses:** Ezekiel 33:11; John 3:16–19; Romans 2:5–11; 2 Peter 3:9

**Tags:** hell, justice, love, apologetics

### 10.5 What is the final judgment?

**Also asked:** Will Christians be judged? · What is the judgment seat of Christ?

**Short answer:** When Christ returns, everyone will stand before God to give an account. Those who trusted in Christ will not be condemned; their works will be evaluated for reward. Those who rejected him will be judged for their sins.

**Explanation:** Romans 8:1 says there is no condemnation for those in Christ. Yet believers will appear before the judgment seat of Christ to receive what is due for their deeds (2 Corinthians 5:10; 1 Corinthians 3:12–15). Revelation 20:11–15 describes the great white throne judgment.

**Key verses:** Matthew 25:31–46; John 5:24–29; Romans 8:1; 2 Corinthians 5:10; Hebrews 9:27; Revelation 20:11–15

**Tags:** judgment, afterlife

### 10.6 What is the resurrection of the body?

**Also asked:** Will we have bodies in heaven?

**Short answer:** Christians believe that at Christ’s return the dead will be raised with real, physical bodies, transformed to be imperishable and glorious, like Jesus’ resurrection body.

**Explanation:** 1 Corinthians 15 explains that the body is sown perishable and raised imperishable. Christianity does not teach escape from the body but its redemption. Jesus’ risen body could be touched and could eat (Luke 24:39–43), a preview of ours.

**Key verses:** Luke 24:36–43; John 5:28–29; 1 Corinthians 15:35–57; Philippians 3:20–21; 1 Thessalonians 4:16

**Tags:** resurrection, afterlife, body

### 10.7 Will we recognise each other in heaven?

**Also asked:** Will I see my loved ones again?

**Short answer:** The Bible gives good reason to believe believers will recognise one another. Those who die in Christ will be reunited with all who belong to him.

**Explanation:** At the transfiguration, the disciples recognised Moses and Elijah (Matthew 17:3–4). Paul comforted grieving believers that they would be together with the Lord and each other (1 Thessalonians 4:13–18). Our relationships will be perfected, not erased.

**Key verses:** 2 Samuel 12:23; Matthew 8:11; Matthew 17:3–4; 1 Thessalonians 4:13–18

**Tags:** heaven, reunion, grief

### 10.8 Will there be marriage in heaven?

**Also asked:** Will I still be married to my spouse in heaven?

**Short answer:** Jesus said that in the resurrection people “neither marry nor are given in marriage” (Matthew 22:30). Marriage points to the ultimate union of Christ and his church, which will be fulfilled then.

**Explanation:** This does not mean love between spouses is lost. Relationships will be deeper and purer, but the institution of marriage will have served its purpose (Ephesians 5:31–32; Revelation 19:7–9).

**Key verses:** Matthew 22:23–30; Ephesians 5:31–32; Revelation 19:7–9

**Tags:** heaven, marriage

### 10.9 Do pets go to heaven?

**Also asked:** Will animals be in heaven?

**Short answer:** The Bible does not directly answer whether individual pets will be in heaven. It does show animals in the vision of the renewed creation, and we can trust God’s goodness.

**Explanation:** Isaiah 11:6–9 pictures animals living in peace in God’s kingdom. Romans 8:19–22 says all creation will be set free from decay. Animals are not described as having eternal souls in the same way as humans, so Christians hold this question with humility and hope.

**Key verses:** Psalm 36:6; Isaiah 11:6–9; Romans 8:19–22

**Tags:** heaven, animals

### 10.10 Is purgatory biblical?

**Also asked:** What do Protestants believe about purgatory?

**Short answer:** Evangelicals do not believe in purgatory. Christ’s sacrifice fully paid for the sins of believers, so no further cleansing by suffering is needed after death.

**Explanation:** Purgatory is Catholic teaching drawn mainly from tradition and the Apocrypha (2 Maccabees 12). Hebrews 10:14 says Christ “has perfected forever” those being sanctified. The thief on the cross was promised Paradise that same day (Luke 23:43).

**Key verses:** Luke 23:43; Romans 8:1; 2 Corinthians 5:8; Hebrews 10:10–18; 1 John 1:7

**Tags:** afterlife, purgatory, Catholicism

### 10.11 Does the Bible teach reincarnation?

**Also asked:** Do we come back in another life?

**Short answer:** No. The Bible teaches that each person lives once, then faces judgment. Christianity teaches resurrection, not reincarnation.

**Explanation:** Hebrews 9:27 says “it is appointed for men to die once, and after this, judgment.” The resurrection is the raising of the same person in a renewed body, not a return in another form.

**Key verses:** 2 Samuel 12:23; Luke 16:19–31; Hebrews 9:27

**Tags:** afterlife, reincarnation, other religions

### 10.12 Are ghosts real? Can the dead contact us?

**Also asked:** Can I talk to my dead relatives? · What about mediums and séances?

**Short answer:** The Bible teaches that the dead do not wander the earth, and it strictly forbids trying to contact them through mediums. Spiritual experiences of this kind may be deceptive.

**Explanation:** Deuteronomy 18:10–12 forbids consulting the dead. In the story of the rich man and Lazarus, there is a fixed separation after death (Luke 16:26). The unusual case of Samuel and the medium of Endor (1 Samuel 28) was an exception God allowed in judgment on Saul.

**Key verses:** Leviticus 19:31; Deuteronomy 18:10–12; 1 Samuel 28; Luke 16:26; 2 Corinthians 11:14

**Tags:** afterlife, ghosts, occult

### 10.13 Can a Christian who takes their own life go to heaven?

**Also asked:** Is suicide an unforgivable sin?

**Short answer:** Taking a life, including one’s own, is wrong, but the Bible does not teach that suicide is unforgivable. Salvation rests on Christ’s finished work, not on a person’s final moment, and many evangelicals believe a true believer who dies this way is with the Lord.

**Explanation:** Nothing can separate those in Christ from God’s love (Romans 8:38–39). Those grieving such a loss need deep compassion, not condemnation. If you are having thoughts of ending your life, please reach out now: in South Africa, call the SADAG Suicide Crisis Helpline on 0800 567 567 (free, 24 hours) or the LifeLine South Africa National Counselling Line on 0861 322 322 (24 hours), or talk to someone you trust. If your life is in immediate danger, contact emergency services. Your life matters to God and to others.

**Key verses:** Psalm 34:18; John 10:28–29; Romans 8:38–39

**Tags:** death, suicide, grief, mental health

### 10.14 Should Christians be cremated or buried?

**Also asked:** Is cremation a sin?

**Short answer:** The Bible does not forbid cremation. Burial was the normal practice for God’s people and pictures the hope of resurrection, but God can raise any body, whatever happened to it.

**Explanation:** Many Christians prefer burial as a testimony to bodily resurrection; others choose cremation for practical reasons. It is a matter of conscience and can be discussed with family and your pastor.

**Key verses:** Genesis 23:19; John 19:40–42; 1 Corinthians 15:42–44

**Tags:** death, cremation, burial

## 11. Angels, Satan and Spiritual Warfare

### 11.1 What are angels?

**Also asked:** Do angels exist? · What do angels do?

**Short answer:** Angels are spiritual beings created by God to worship him and serve as his messengers. They help and protect God’s people, but they are not to be worshipped or prayed to.

**Explanation:** Hebrews 1:14 calls angels “ministering spirits” sent to serve those who will inherit salvation. Angels announced Jesus’ birth and resurrection. When John tried to worship an angel, he was told, “Worship God” (Revelation 22:8–9). Named angels include Michael and Gabriel.

**Key verses:** Psalm 91:11; Psalm 103:20–21; Luke 1:26–38; Hebrews 1:14; Revelation 22:8–9

**Tags:** angels

### 11.2 Do people become angels when they die?

**Also asked:** Is my loved one an angel now?

**Short answer:** No. Angels and humans are different kinds of beings. Believers who die do not become angels; they are with the Lord and will be raised as glorified humans.

**Explanation:** Humans alone are made in God’s image, and believers will even judge angels (1 Corinthians 6:3). Jesus said resurrected people will be “like” angels in not marrying, not that they become angels (Matthew 22:30).

**Key verses:** Matthew 22:30; 1 Corinthians 6:3; Hebrews 2:16; Hebrews 12:22–23

**Tags:** angels, death, afterlife

### 11.3 Do we have guardian angels?

**Also asked:** Does everyone have a guardian angel?

**Short answer:** The Bible teaches that angels protect and serve God’s people, but it does not clearly teach that each person has one specific guardian angel. Our trust is in God himself.

**Explanation:** Psalm 91:11 says God commands his angels to guard his people. Matthew 18:10 mentions the angels of “these little ones,” which some read as personal guardian angels. Christians may hold different views on the details.

**Key verses:** Psalm 34:7; Psalm 91:11–12; Matthew 18:10; Acts 12:15; Hebrews 1:14

**Tags:** angels, protection

### 11.4 Who is Satan?

**Also asked:** Is the devil real? · Where did Satan come from?

**Short answer:** Satan is a real, personal, fallen angel who rebelled against God and leads other fallen angels (demons). He is the enemy of God and his people, but he is a created being, far weaker than God, and already defeated by Christ.

**Explanation:** Satan means “adversary.” He is called the father of lies (John 8:44) and the tempter. Many see Isaiah 14 and Ezekiel 28 as alluding to his fall through pride, though interpreters differ. Jesus came to destroy the devil’s works (1 John 3:8), and Satan’s final defeat is certain (Revelation 20:10).

**Key verses:** Genesis 3:1–5; Job 1–2; John 8:44; 2 Corinthians 11:14; 1 Peter 5:8–9; 1 John 3:8; Revelation 12:9; Revelation 20:10

**Tags:** Satan, devil, spiritual warfare

### 11.5 What are demons?

**Also asked:** Are demons real? · Where do demons come from?

**Short answer:** Demons are fallen angels who joined Satan’s rebellion. They oppose God and seek to deceive and harm people, but they are subject to Christ’s authority.

**Explanation:** The Gospels show Jesus casting out demons with a word, and they recognised who he was (Mark 1:23–27). 2 Peter 2:4 and Jude 6 speak of angels who sinned. Believers need not fear them, because “greater is he who is in you than he who is in the world” (1 John 4:4).

**Key verses:** Mark 1:23–27; Luke 10:17–20; Ephesians 6:12; James 2:19; 2 Peter 2:4; 1 John 4:4

**Tags:** demons, spiritual warfare

### 11.6 Can a Christian be demon-possessed?

**Also asked:** Can demons control Christians?

**Short answer:** Most evangelicals believe a true Christian cannot be possessed (owned and controlled) by a demon, because the Holy Spirit lives in them. Christians can, however, be tempted, attacked and influenced by evil.

**Explanation:** Believers have been rescued from the domain of darkness (Colossians 1:13) and are sealed by the Spirit (Ephesians 1:13). Some evangelicals use the word “demonisation” to describe serious spiritual oppression of believers. All agree the answer is to resist the devil and draw near to God (James 4:7).

**Key verses:** 1 Corinthians 6:19–20; Ephesians 1:13; Colossians 1:13; James 4:7; 1 John 4:4; 1 John 5:18

**Tags:** demons, spiritual warfare, Christian life

### 11.7 What is spiritual warfare?

**Also asked:** How do I fight spiritual battles? · What is the armour of God?

**Short answer:** Spiritual warfare is the Christian’s struggle against sin, the world’s pressures and the devil. Christians stand firm using the “armour of God”: truth, righteousness, the gospel, faith, salvation, God’s word and prayer.

**Explanation:** Ephesians 6:10–18 says our struggle is not against flesh and blood but against spiritual forces of evil. The battle is mainly fought through trusting God, obeying his word and praying, not through special rituals. Christ has already won the decisive victory (Colossians 2:15).

**Key verses:** 2 Corinthians 10:3–5; Ephesians 6:10–18; Colossians 2:15; James 4:7; 1 Peter 5:8–9

**Tags:** spiritual warfare, armour of God

### 11.8 Is it wrong to use horoscopes, tarot or fortune tellers?

**Also asked:** What does the Bible say about astrology and the occult?

**Short answer:** Yes. The Bible forbids seeking guidance through divination, astrology, mediums and similar practices, because they seek hidden knowledge apart from God and can open people to deception.

**Explanation:** Deuteronomy 18:10–12 lists these practices as detestable to God. In Acts 19:18–19 new believers burned their magic books. Christians look to God and his word for guidance about the future.

**Key verses:** Leviticus 19:26, 31; Deuteronomy 18:10–14; Isaiah 47:13–14; Acts 16:16–18; Acts 19:18–19

**Tags:** occult, astrology, spiritual warfare

### 11.9 Can Satan read my mind?

**Also asked:** Does the devil know everything?

**Short answer:** The Bible never says Satan can read minds. Only God knows all things and searches the heart. Satan is powerful but limited; he is not all-knowing, all-powerful or everywhere present.

**Explanation:** God alone knows the human heart (1 Kings 8:39; Psalm 139). Satan can observe behaviour and tempt, but he cannot do anything God does not permit (Job 1:12).

**Key verses:** 1 Kings 8:39; Job 1:12; Psalm 139:1–4; Jeremiah 17:10

**Tags:** Satan, spiritual warfare

### 11.10 Why doesn’t God destroy Satan now?

**Also asked:** Why does God allow the devil to exist?

**Short answer:** Satan’s defeat is certain and already secured at the cross, but God has chosen a set time for his final judgment. Until then, God uses even Satan’s schemes for his purposes.

**Explanation:** Revelation 20:10 describes Satan’s final end. In the meantime, God shows his wisdom and patience, giving people time to repent (2 Peter 3:9). As with the problem of evil, we may not know every reason, but we know the outcome.

**Key verses:** Genesis 3:15; Romans 16:20; Colossians 2:15; 2 Peter 3:9; Revelation 20:10

**Tags:** Satan, problem of evil, end times

### 11.11 What are cherubim and seraphim?

**Also asked:** What are the different kinds of angels? · Who is Michael the archangel?

**Short answer:** Cherubim and seraphim are kinds of angelic beings described in Scripture. Cherubim guard God’s holiness; seraphim worship around his throne. Michael is called an archangel, a chief angel.

**Explanation:** Cherubim guarded the way to the tree of life (Genesis 3:24) and were pictured on the ark of the covenant. Seraphim appear in Isaiah 6, crying “Holy, holy, holy.” Michael is described as contending against Satan (Jude 9; Revelation 12:7).

**Key verses:** Genesis 3:24; Exodus 25:18–22; Isaiah 6:1–7; Daniel 10:13; Jude 9; Revelation 12:7

**Tags:** angels, cherubim, seraphim

## 12. End Times

### 12.1 What does the Bible say about the end of the world?

**Also asked:** What is eschatology? · How will the world end?

**Short answer:** The Bible teaches that history is heading toward a goal: Jesus will return, the dead will be raised, God will judge the world, and he will create a new heaven and new earth where his people live with him forever.

**Explanation:** All evangelicals share these core hopes. They differ on details such as the timing of the rapture, the meaning of the millennium and how to read the symbols in Revelation (see 12.3–12.5). The main purpose of end-times teaching is to give hope and encourage faithful, watchful living (2 Peter 3:11–14).

**Key verses:** Matthew 24–25; Acts 1:11; 1 Thessalonians 4:13–5:11; 2 Peter 3:10–14; Revelation 21–22

**Tags:** end times, eschatology

### 12.2 When will Jesus return?

**Also asked:** Can we know the date of the second coming? · Are we living in the last days?

**Short answer:** No one knows the day or hour, not even the angels. Jesus told his followers to be ready at all times. Anyone who sets a date is going against Jesus’ own words.

**Explanation:** Jesus said, “But no one knows of that day and hour” (Matthew 24:36). The New Testament calls the whole period since Jesus’ first coming “the last days” (Hebrews 1:2; Acts 2:17). Many predictions throughout history have failed. The right response is faithful readiness, not speculation.

**Key verses:** Matthew 24:36–44; Mark 13:32–37; Acts 1:6–7; 1 Thessalonians 5:1–6; Hebrews 1:2

**Tags:** end times, second coming

### 12.3 What is the rapture?

**Also asked:** Will Christians be raptured before the tribulation?

**Short answer:** The rapture refers to believers being “caught up” to meet the Lord when he returns (1 Thessalonians 4:17). Evangelicals agree this will happen but differ on its timing.

**Contested among evangelicals.** Pre-tribulation believers (common among dispensationalists) hold that Christ will take the church before a seven-year tribulation. Mid- and pre-wrath views place it partway through. Post-tribulation believers hold that the church will be gathered at Christ’s single, visible return after the tribulation. Many amillennial and postmillennial Christians see the “catching up” as part of the one second coming. Speak with your local pastor for more.

**Key verses:** Matthew 24:29–31; John 14:1–3; 1 Corinthians 15:51–52; 1 Thessalonians 4:13–18; 2 Thessalonians 2:1–4

**Tags:** end times, rapture, contested

### 12.4 What is the millennium?

**Also asked:** What is the thousand-year reign? · Premillennial, amillennial or postmillennial?

**Short answer:** Revelation 20 speaks of Christ reigning for a thousand years. Evangelicals agree Christ reigns and will return, but they interpret this passage in different ways.

**Contested among evangelicals.** Premillennialists believe Christ will return before a literal future reign on earth. Amillennialists understand the thousand years symbolically as Christ’s present reign from heaven between his first and second comings. Postmillennialists believe the gospel will spread and bring a long era of blessing before Christ returns. Speak with your local pastor for more.

**Key verses:** Isaiah 65:17–25; Revelation 20:1–10

**Tags:** end times, millennium, contested

### 12.5 What is the tribulation?

**Also asked:** Will Christians go through the great tribulation?

**Short answer:** The Bible speaks of times of great distress before Christ’s return. Evangelicals differ on whether this refers to a specific future seven-year period, to the destruction of Jerusalem in AD 70, to the whole church age, or a combination.

**Contested among evangelicals.** Dispensational interpreters see a future seven-year tribulation based on Daniel 9:24–27. Others see much of Jesus’ Olivet Discourse fulfilled in AD 70, and the “tribulation” as the suffering the church faces throughout history (John 16:33; Revelation 7:14). All agree Christians are called to endure faithfully. Speak with your local pastor for more.

**Key verses:** Daniel 9:24–27; Matthew 24:15–31; John 16:33; Revelation 7:14

**Tags:** end times, tribulation, contested

### 12.6 Who is the Antichrist?

**Also asked:** Is the Antichrist alive today? · What is the mark of the beast?

**Short answer:** The Bible speaks of a coming “man of lawlessness” who opposes God, and also of “many antichrists”: anyone who denies that Jesus is the Christ. Christians should be cautious about identifying any modern person as the Antichrist.

**Explanation:** 1 John 2:18–22 describes the spirit of antichrist already at work. 2 Thessalonians 2:3–4 speaks of a final figure. The “mark of the beast” (Revelation 13) is interpreted in different ways, but it is about ultimate allegiance: belonging to the beast rather than to God. No one can receive it by accident.

**Key verses:** Daniel 7:23–26; 2 Thessalonians 2:1–12; 1 John 2:18–22; 1 John 4:3; Revelation 13

**Tags:** end times, Antichrist

### 12.7 How should I read the book of Revelation?

**Also asked:** Is Revelation literal or symbolic?

**Short answer:** Revelation is a prophecy written in apocalyptic style, full of symbols drawn from the Old Testament. Its central message is clear: Jesus has won, God will judge evil, and his people should remain faithful.

**Explanation:** It was first written to seven real churches in Asia Minor facing pressure (Revelation 1–3). Interpreters differ on how much refers to the past, the whole church age or the future. Reading it alongside the Old Testament helps decode its imagery. It promises a blessing to those who read and obey it (Revelation 1:3).

**Key verses:** Revelation 1:1–3, 19; Revelation 5; Revelation 21–22

**Tags:** end times, Revelation, interpretation

### 12.8 What will the new heaven and new earth be like?

**Also asked:** Will God destroy the earth?

**Short answer:** God will renew and restore his creation, removing all sin and its effects. The new creation will be a real, physical world where God lives with his people forever.

**Explanation:** Romans 8:21 says creation will be set free from bondage to decay. Revelation 21:5 says God makes “all things new.” Some passages speak of the old order passing away by fire (2 Peter 3:10–13), which many understand as purifying rather than annihilating the world.

**Key verses:** Isaiah 65:17–25; Romans 8:19–23; 2 Peter 3:10–13; Revelation 21:1–5; Revelation 22:1–5

**Tags:** end times, new creation, heaven

### 12.9 What does the Bible say about Israel and the end times?

**Also asked:** Does modern Israel fulfil prophecy?

**Short answer:** Evangelicals agree God has not forgotten the Jewish people and that salvation for Jews and Gentiles alike is through Jesus the Messiah. They differ on the role of the modern state of Israel in prophecy.

**Contested among evangelicals.** Dispensationalists see God’s promises to ethnic Israel, including the land, as still awaiting fulfilment, and often view Israel’s 1948 founding as prophetically significant. Covenant theologians see the promises fulfilled in Christ and the church of Jews and Gentiles, while many still expect a future turning of Jewish people to Christ (Romans 11:25–27). Speak with your local pastor for more.

**Key verses:** Genesis 12:1–3; Jeremiah 31:31–34; Romans 9–11; Galatians 3:7–29; Ephesians 2:11–22

**Tags:** end times, Israel, contested

### 12.10 How should the end times affect how I live?

**Also asked:** Should I be afraid of the end times?

**Short answer:** The Bible’s teaching on the end is meant to bring hope, not fear. It calls believers to live holy lives, share the gospel, be watchful and comfort one another with the promise of Christ’s return.

**Explanation:** After describing the Lord’s coming, Paul says, “therefore comfort one another with these words” (1 Thessalonians 4:18). Peter asks what kind of people we ought to be in light of these things (2 Peter 3:11). Christians can look forward to Christ’s return with joy: “Come, Lord Jesus” (Revelation 22:20).

**Key verses:** Matthew 25:1–13; 1 Thessalonians 4:18; Titus 2:11–14; 2 Peter 3:11–14; 1 John 3:2–3; Revelation 22:20

**Tags:** end times, hope, Christian living

## 13. Common Objections

### 13.1 Doesn’t science disprove God?

**Also asked:** Are science and Christianity in conflict? · Can a scientist believe in God?

**Short answer:** No. Science studies how the natural world works; it cannot answer whether God exists or why there is a universe at all. Many leading scientists, past and present, have been Christians.

**Explanation:** Modern science grew largely in a Christian setting, with figures like Kepler, Boyle, Newton, Faraday and Maxwell believing they were studying God’s ordered creation. Today, Christian scientists such as geneticist Francis Collins argue that faith and science complement each other. Discoveries like the universe having a beginning and being finely tuned for life are seen by many as pointing toward a Creator.

**Key verses:** Psalm 19:1–4; Psalm 111:2; Romans 1:20; Colossians 1:16–17

**Tags:** apologetics, science

### 13.2 Aren’t all religions basically the same?

**Also asked:** Don’t all roads lead to God? · Isn’t it arrogant to say Christianity is the only truth?

**Short answer:** Religions share some moral teachings, but they make contradictory claims about God, Jesus, salvation and the afterlife, so they cannot all be true. Christianity is unique in teaching salvation as God’s free gift through Jesus’ death and resurrection.

**Explanation:** For example, Christianity says Jesus died and rose; Islam says he was not crucified. Both cannot be correct. Saying Jesus is the only way is not arrogance if it is true and if the message is offered humbly to everyone; Christians did not invent it, Jesus said it (John 14:6). Christians are called to treat people of all faiths with respect and love.

**Key verses:** John 14:6; Acts 4:12; Acts 17:22–31; 1 Peter 3:15

**Tags:** apologetics, other religions, exclusivity

### 13.3 Why are there so many hypocrites in the church?

**Also asked:** Why do Christians act so badly? · Why should I believe when Christians have done harm?

**Short answer:** The church is made of forgiven sinners, not perfect people. Hypocrisy is real and wrong, and Jesus himself condemned it strongly. But the failures of Christians do not disprove Jesus; they show why we all need him.

**Explanation:** Jesus reserved some of his harshest words for religious hypocrites (Matthew 23). Where Christians have caused harm, including abuse, they must repent and make things right. The best measure of Christianity is Jesus himself, and the many believers who have served others sacrificially.

**Key verses:** Matthew 7:21–23; Matthew 23; Romans 3:23; 1 John 1:8–9

**Tags:** apologetics, hypocrisy, church

### 13.4 Isn’t faith just believing without evidence?

**Also asked:** Is Christianity irrational? · Is faith blind?

**Short answer:** No. Biblical faith is trust based on good reasons: the evidence of creation, the historical evidence for Jesus’ life and resurrection, fulfilled prophecy and the reliability of Scripture.

**Explanation:** Luke wrote so his readers would have certainty (Luke 1:1–4). Peter told Christians to be ready to give reasons for their hope (1 Peter 3:15). Everyone trusts in something they cannot prove absolutely; the question is whether that trust is well-founded.

**Key verses:** Luke 1:1–4; John 20:30–31; Acts 17:2–3; 1 Corinthians 15:3–8; 1 Peter 3:15

**Tags:** apologetics, faith, reason

### 13.5 Didn’t Christianity copy pagan myths?

**Also asked:** Is Jesus based on Horus or Mithras?

**Short answer:** No. Historians, including non-Christian scholars, overwhelmingly reject the claim that the story of Jesus was copied from pagan myths. The supposed parallels are usually exaggerated, late or invented.

**Explanation:** The Gospels are rooted in first-century Jewish history with real places and people. Dying-and-rising god myths are very different from a bodily resurrection of a historical person witnessed by many. Many claimed parallels come from popular internet sources rather than ancient texts.

**Key verses:** Luke 1:1–4; 2 Peter 1:16; 1 Corinthians 15:3–8

**Tags:** apologetics, history, Jesus

### 13.6 Doesn’t the Bible support slavery?

**Also asked:** Why does the Bible mention slavery?

**Short answer:** The Bible regulates practices that existed in the ancient world, but it does not endorse the race-based chattel slavery of later centuries. It condemns kidnapping people to sell them, and its teaching that all people bear God’s image and are one in Christ undermined slavery from within.

**Explanation:** Exodus 21:16 and 1 Timothy 1:10 condemn man-stealing. Ancient Israelite servitude was often a form of debt repayment with protections and release. Paul urged Philemon to receive Onesimus “no longer as a slave” but as a brother. Christians like William Wilberforce were leaders in abolishing the slave trade, although others wrongly used the Bible to defend it.

**Key verses:** Genesis 1:27; Exodus 21:16; Deuteronomy 23:15–16; Galatians 3:28; 1 Timothy 1:10; Philemon 15–16

**Tags:** apologetics, slavery, ethics

### 13.7 Why did God command violence in the Old Testament?

**Also asked:** Why did God order the destruction of the Canaanites?

**Short answer:** These are hard passages. The Bible presents them as God’s specific, limited judgment on nations for long-standing, extreme evil (including child sacrifice), after centuries of patience. They are not a general command for believers today.

**Explanation:** God told Abraham the judgment would wait because the sin of the Amorites was “not yet full” (Genesis 15:16). God also judged Israel when it did the same evils. Ancient war language often used hyperbole. Christians are commanded to love their enemies (Matthew 5:44), and the church does not wage holy war. Such passages call for careful study, humility and trust in God’s just character.

**Key verses:** Genesis 15:16; Leviticus 18:24–28; Deuteronomy 9:4–5; Ezekiel 18:23; Matthew 5:44

**Tags:** apologetics, Old Testament, violence, justice

### 13.8 If God is real, why doesn’t he show himself?

**Also asked:** Why is God hidden? · Why can’t I see God?

**Short answer:** Christians believe God has shown himself: through creation, conscience, Scripture and supremely by coming in person as Jesus. Many who saw Jesus’ miracles still refused to believe, which shows the issue is often the heart, not the evidence.

**Explanation:** Jesus said that those who will not listen to Scripture would not be convinced even if someone rose from the dead (Luke 16:31). God promises to be found by those who seek him with all their heart (Jeremiah 29:13).

**Key verses:** Jeremiah 29:13; Luke 16:31; John 1:18; John 14:8–9; Romans 1:19–20; Hebrews 11:6

**Tags:** apologetics, hiddenness of God

### 13.9 Hasn’t religion caused most wars?

**Also asked:** Isn’t religion the cause of violence?

**Short answer:** Religion has sometimes been used to justify violence, and Christians must own those failures. But historians note that most wars have had political, territorial or economic causes, and some of the worst violence of the twentieth century came from openly atheistic regimes.

**Explanation:** Violence in Jesus’ name contradicts his teaching to love enemies and put away the sword (Matthew 26:52). Christianity has also inspired hospitals, schools, charities and movements for justice. The root problem is the human heart (James 4:1–2).

**Key verses:** Matthew 5:9, 43–45; Matthew 26:52; Romans 12:17–21; James 4:1–2

**Tags:** apologetics, violence, history

### 13.10 Can I be good without God?

**Also asked:** Do I need God to be moral?

**Short answer:** People who do not believe in God can do good things, because everyone is made in God’s image with a conscience. The deeper question is where moral standards come from: Christians argue that objective right and wrong make most sense if there is a moral Lawgiver.

**Explanation:** Romans 2:14–15 says God’s law is written on human hearts. But being “good” by human standards is different from being righteous before a holy God, which no one achieves on their own (Romans 3:10).

**Key verses:** Micah 6:8; Romans 2:14–15; Romans 3:10–12, 23

**Tags:** apologetics, morality

### 13.11 Was the Bible written by men, so how can it be God’s word?

**Also asked:** Didn’t people just make up the Bible?

**Short answer:** The Bible was written by human authors, but Christians believe God guided them so that what they wrote is his word (see 4.2). Its unity across many authors and centuries, fulfilled prophecy and transforming power support this claim.

**Explanation:** Jesus treated the Scriptures as the word of God (Matthew 5:18; John 10:35). The New Testament was written by eyewitnesses and their close associates, many of whom suffered for what they wrote.

**Key verses:** Matthew 5:18; John 10:35; 2 Timothy 3:16; 2 Peter 1:16–21

**Tags:** apologetics, Bible, inspiration

### 13.12 Doesn’t Christianity oppress women?

**Also asked:** Is the Bible sexist?

**Short answer:** The Bible teaches that women and men are equally made in God’s image and equally redeemed. In a culture that often disregarded women, Jesus treated them with dignity, taught them and chose women as the first witnesses of his resurrection.

**Explanation:** Women such as Mary Magdalene, Martha and Mary, Priscilla and Phoebe played important roles in Jesus’ ministry and the early church. Where Christians have mistreated women, they have contradicted Scripture. Evangelicals differ on certain roles in church leadership (see 8.11), but all affirm equal worth.

**Key verses:** Genesis 1:27; Luke 8:1–3; Luke 10:38–42; John 20:11–18; Galatians 3:28; 1 Peter 3:7

**Tags:** apologetics, women, gender

### 13.13 What about people who say they had a different religious experience?

**Also asked:** How do I know my religious experience is from God?

**Short answer:** Experiences are real and can be powerful, but they must be tested by Scripture. Christianity rests not on private feelings but on public historical events, above all Jesus’ resurrection.

**Explanation:** 1 John 4:1 says to test the spirits. Paul warned that even an angel preaching a different gospel should be rejected (Galatians 1:8). Experiences that point to the Jesus of the Bible and produce godly fruit are good signs.

**Key verses:** Galatians 1:6–9; 2 Corinthians 11:14; 1 Thessalonians 5:21; 1 John 4:1–3

**Tags:** apologetics, experience, discernment

### 13.14 Are Jehovah’s Witnesses and Mormons Christians?

**Also asked:** What do Jehovah’s Witnesses believe? · What do Latter-day Saints believe?

**Short answer:** Evangelicals respect members of these groups as people, but do not consider their teachings Christian in the historic sense, because they deny core doctrines such as the Trinity and the full deity of Jesus.

**Explanation:** Jehovah’s Witnesses teach that Jesus is a created being (Michael the archangel) and deny the Trinity. The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints teaches that God the Father has a physical body, and adds other scriptures to the Bible. These conflict with the Bible and the historic creeds (see 1.3 and 2.2). Christians are called to engage them with gentleness and respect.

**Key verses:** John 1:1–3; John 8:58; Galatians 1:6–9; Colossians 2:9; 1 John 4:1–3

**Tags:** apologetics, other religions, cults

### 13.15 Why does Christianity have so many rules?

**Also asked:** Isn’t Christianity just a list of dos and don’ts?

**Short answer:** Christianity is not mainly about rules but about a relationship with God through Jesus. God’s commands are given for our good, and Christians obey out of love and gratitude, not to earn salvation.

**Explanation:** Jesus summarised the law as loving God and loving others (Matthew 22:37–40). 1 John 5:3 says his commands are not burdensome. Like a loving parent’s instructions, God’s commands protect us and lead to true freedom (John 8:31–36).

**Key verses:** Deuteronomy 10:12–13; Matthew 11:28–30; Matthew 22:37–40; John 8:31–36; John 14:15; 1 John 5:3

**Tags:** apologetics, law, grace, Christian living

### 13.16 Can I be spiritual but not religious?

**Also asked:** Do I need organised religion?

**Short answer:** Many people sense a spiritual hunger, which Christians see as a sign that we were made for God. But Jesus does not offer vague spirituality; he invites people to know him personally and to belong to his people, the church.

**Explanation:** Augustine wrote that our hearts are restless until they rest in God. Jesus said, “Come to me” (Matthew 11:28), not merely to an idea. Spirituality that ignores the truth about God and sin cannot meet our deepest need.

**Key verses:** Ecclesiastes 3:11; Matthew 11:28–30; John 4:13–14; John 14:6; Acts 17:24–28

**Tags:** apologetics, spirituality, church

## 14. Christian Living

### 14.1 How should a Christian live?

**Also asked:** What does God want from me? · What is the Christian life?

**Short answer:** A Christian lives by loving God with all their heart and loving their neighbour as themselves, following Jesus in daily obedience, empowered by the Holy Spirit and motivated by gratitude for grace.

**Explanation:** Micah 6:8 summarises: do justly, love mercy, walk humbly with God. Christians are called to be “salt” and “light” (Matthew 5:13–16), to put off old sinful habits and put on Christlike ones (Colossians 3:1–17), and to do everything for God’s glory.

**Key verses:** Micah 6:8; Matthew 5:13–16; Matthew 22:37–40; Romans 12:1–2; Colossians 3:1–17

**Tags:** Christian living, discipleship

### 14.2 What are the Ten Commandments?

**Also asked:** Do Christians still keep the Ten Commandments?

**Short answer:** The Ten Commandments, given at Mount Sinai (Exodus 20; Deuteronomy 5), are: have no other gods; make no idols; do not misuse God’s name; keep the Sabbath; honour your parents; do not murder; do not commit adultery; do not steal; do not give false testimony; do not covet.

**Explanation:** They reflect God’s unchanging moral character. Jesus deepened them, showing they concern the heart, not just outward actions (Matthew 5:21–28). Christians do not keep them to be saved, but they remain a guide to loving God and neighbour. Nine of the ten are repeated in the New Testament; the Sabbath is treated differently (see 8.13).

**Key verses:** Exodus 20:1–17; Deuteronomy 5:6–21; Matthew 5:17–28; Romans 13:8–10

**Tags:** Ten Commandments, law, ethics

### 14.3 How do I forgive someone who hurt me?

**Also asked:** Do I have to forgive? · Does forgiveness mean reconciling?

**Short answer:** Christians are called to forgive because God has forgiven them in Christ. Forgiveness means releasing the debt and the desire for revenge, leaving justice to God. It does not mean pretending the wrong didn’t happen, and it does not always mean restoring trust or staying in a harmful situation.

**Explanation:** Jesus taught us to pray “forgive us our debts, as we also forgive our debtors” (Matthew 6:12). Forgiveness is often a process and may need to be chosen repeatedly. Reconciliation requires repentance and rebuilt trust. In cases of abuse, safety comes first; forgiving does not mean staying in danger or not reporting crimes.

**Key verses:** Matthew 6:12–15; Matthew 18:21–35; Romans 12:17–21; Ephesians 4:31–32; Colossians 3:13

**Tags:** forgiveness, relationships

### 14.4 How do I overcome temptation and sin?

**Also asked:** Why do I keep sinning? · How do I break a bad habit?

**Short answer:** Every Christian still struggles with sin. God promises a way of escape in every temptation. Victory comes through relying on the Spirit, knowing God’s word, avoiding situations that lead to sin, being accountable to others and confessing quickly when you fall.

**Explanation:** Paul describes the inner struggle in Romans 7 and the Spirit’s power in Romans 8. Jesus resisted temptation with Scripture (Matthew 4:1–11). 1 John 1:9 promises forgiveness when we confess. Struggling is not proof you are not a Christian; caring about sin is a sign of the Spirit’s work.

**Key verses:** Psalm 119:11; Matthew 4:1–11; Romans 6:11–14; 1 Corinthians 10:13; Galatians 5:16; James 4:7; 1 John 1:9

**Tags:** temptation, sin, sanctification

### 14.5 What does the Bible say about sex before marriage?

**Also asked:** Is sex before marriage a sin? · Is living together okay?

**Short answer:** The Bible teaches that sex is God’s good gift designed for marriage between a husband and wife. Sexual activity outside marriage is called sexual immorality, and Christians are called to sexual purity.

**Explanation:** 1 Corinthians 6:18–20 says to flee sexual immorality because our bodies belong to God. 1 Thessalonians 4:3–5 links God’s will to self-control. For anyone with past sexual sin, the gospel offers complete forgiveness and a fresh start (1 Corinthians 6:11).

**Key verses:** Genesis 2:24; 1 Corinthians 6:9–20; 1 Corinthians 7:2–9; 1 Thessalonians 4:3–8; Hebrews 13:4

**Tags:** sexuality, marriage, ethics

### 14.6 What is God’s design for marriage?

**Also asked:** What does the Bible say about husbands and wives?

**Short answer:** Marriage is a lifelong covenant between one man and one woman, created by God for companionship, mutual love and support, sexual union and, usually, children. It also pictures Christ’s love for the church.

**Explanation:** Jesus affirmed Genesis 2:24 when asked about marriage (Matthew 19:4–6). Ephesians 5:21–33 calls husbands to love sacrificially as Christ loved the church and wives to respect and support their husbands. Evangelicals differ on how roles of headship and submission apply in practice (see 8.11 for the related debate).

**Key verses:** Genesis 2:18–25; Malachi 2:14–16; Matthew 19:4–6; Ephesians 5:21–33; 1 Peter 3:1–7

**Tags:** marriage, family, relationships

### 14.7 What does the Bible say about divorce and remarriage?

**Also asked:** Is divorce a sin? · Can a divorced Christian remarry?

**Short answer:** God intends marriage to be permanent and hates the harm divorce causes. Most evangelicals recognise biblical grounds for divorce in some situations, but they differ on what those grounds are and when remarriage is allowed.

**Contested among evangelicals.** Many evangelicals hold that divorce is permitted for sexual unfaithfulness (Matthew 19:9) and desertion by an unbelieving spouse (1 Corinthians 7:15), with remarriage allowed in those cases. Some also include serious abuse. Others hold a stricter view that remarriage is not permitted while a former spouse lives. Divorce is not an unforgivable sin, and divorced people should be welcomed with grace. Anyone in an abusive situation should seek safety first. Speak with your local pastor for more.

**Key verses:** Malachi 2:16; Matthew 5:31–32; Matthew 19:3–9; 1 Corinthians 7:10–16

**Tags:** divorce, remarriage, marriage, contested

### 14.8 What does the Bible say about homosexuality?

**Also asked:** Is being gay a sin? · Can a gay person be a Christian?

**Short answer:** Evangelicals hold the historic Christian view that the Bible reserves sexual relationships for marriage between a man and a woman, so same-sex sexual activity is outside God’s design. Experiencing same-sex attraction is not itself the same as acting on it, and every person is loved by God and welcome to come to Christ.

**Explanation:** Passages often cited include Leviticus 18:22, Romans 1:26–27 and 1 Corinthians 6:9–11, read alongside Jesus’ affirmation of marriage in Matthew 19:4–6. 1 Corinthians 6:11 also says “such were some of you,” showing that all sinners can be washed and made new. Christians are called to speak truthfully and lovingly, rejecting hatred and mistreatment of anyone. Single Christians, whatever their attractions, are called to the same sexual faithfulness. Some churches and Christians read these passages differently, but this is the historic teaching of the church and the view of this ministry. If this question is personal, speak with a pastor you trust.

**Key verses:** Genesis 2:24; Leviticus 18:22; Matthew 19:4–6; Romans 1:26–27; 1 Corinthians 6:9–11

**Tags:** sexuality, ethics, homosexuality

### 14.9 Can a Christian date or marry a non-Christian?

**Also asked:** What does “unequally yoked” mean?

**Short answer:** The Bible teaches that Christians should marry only fellow believers, because marriage is meant to be a partnership centred on Christ. Someone already married to an unbeliever should stay married if the spouse is willing.

**Explanation:** Paul says a widow may marry whom she wishes, “only in the Lord” (1 Corinthians 7:39). 2 Corinthians 6:14 warns against being “unequally yoked” with unbelievers. For believers already married to non-Christians, 1 Corinthians 7:12–16 and 1 Peter 3:1–2 encourage faithfulness and a godly example.

**Key verses:** 1 Corinthians 7:12–16, 39; 2 Corinthians 6:14–16; 1 Peter 3:1–2

**Tags:** marriage, dating, relationships

### 14.10 Is it a sin for a Christian to drink alcohol?

**Also asked:** Did Jesus drink wine? · Should Christians avoid alcohol?

**Short answer:** The Bible condemns drunkenness but does not forbid all drinking of alcohol. Evangelicals differ on whether moderate drinking or total abstinence is the wiser path.

**Contested among evangelicals.** Many hold that moderate drinking is permitted, noting Jesus turned water into wine (John 2) and Psalm 104:15 speaks of wine gladdening the heart. Others choose or teach abstinence because of the dangers of addiction, the harm alcohol causes and the call not to cause others to stumble (Romans 14:21). Both views agree drunkenness is sin (Ephesians 5:18) and that those with addiction struggles or legal restrictions should abstain. Speak with your local pastor for more.

**Key verses:** Psalm 104:15; Proverbs 20:1; John 2:1–11; Romans 14:21; Ephesians 5:18; 1 Timothy 5:23

**Tags:** alcohol, Christian liberty, contested

### 14.11 What does the Bible say about money?

**Also asked:** Is it wrong to be rich? · Is money the root of all evil?

**Short answer:** Money itself is not evil; the love of money is “a root of all kinds of evil” (1 Timothy 6:10). Christians are called to work honestly, avoid greed, be content, give generously and see possessions as entrusted by God.

**Explanation:** Jesus warned that you cannot serve both God and money (Matthew 6:24) and told the rich to be rich in good works (1 Timothy 6:17–19). Wise planning, saving and avoiding harmful debt are encouraged (Proverbs 21:5; 22:7).

**Key verses:** Proverbs 21:5; Proverbs 22:7; Matthew 6:19–24; Luke 12:15–21; Philippians 4:11–13; 1 Timothy 6:6–10, 17–19

**Tags:** money, stewardship, giving

### 14.12 What does the Bible say about work?

**Also asked:** Does God care about my job? · Is work a curse?

**Short answer:** Work is a good gift from God, given before the fall. Christians are called to work diligently and honestly “as for the Lord,” whatever their job, and to rest as well.

**Explanation:** God placed Adam in the garden to work it (Genesis 2:15); the fall made work harder but not meaningless (Genesis 3:17–19). Colossians 3:23–24 calls believers to work wholeheartedly for the Lord. Every honest job can honour God and serve others.

**Key verses:** Genesis 2:15; Proverbs 6:6–11; Ecclesiastes 3:13; Colossians 3:23–24; 2 Thessalonians 3:10–12

**Tags:** work, vocation

### 14.13 Is anger a sin?

**Also asked:** How should a Christian handle anger?

**Short answer:** Anger is not always sinful; God himself is angry at evil and Jesus was angry at injustice. But human anger easily becomes sinful when it is selfish, uncontrolled, or held as bitterness.

**Explanation:** Ephesians 4:26 says “Be angry, and don’t sin,” and not to let the sun go down on your anger. James 1:19–20 urges being slow to anger. Bring anger to God honestly, deal with conflict quickly and seek help if anger is harming others.

**Key verses:** Psalm 4:4; Proverbs 15:1; Mark 3:5; Ephesians 4:26–32; James 1:19–20

**Tags:** anger, emotions, relationships

### 14.14 How do I share my faith?

**Also asked:** How do I tell my friends about Jesus? · What is evangelism?

**Short answer:** Share your faith by living a life that reflects Christ, praying for people, building genuine friendships and explaining the gospel simply and respectfully when there are opportunities. Your own story of what Jesus has done for you is a good starting point.

**Explanation:** Jesus commanded his followers to make disciples (Matthew 28:19–20). Peter says to always be ready to give a reason for your hope, “with gentleness and respect” (1 Peter 3:15). It is God who changes hearts; our task is to be faithful witnesses.

**Key verses:** Matthew 5:16; Matthew 28:18–20; Acts 1:8; Romans 1:16; Colossians 4:5–6; 1 Peter 3:15

**Tags:** evangelism, witness, Great Commission

### 14.15 Do Christians have to follow the Old Testament law?

**Also asked:** Why don’t Christians keep kosher? · Why follow some Old Testament laws but not others?

**Short answer:** Christians are not under the Old Testament law as a covenant, because Jesus fulfilled it. The moral principles it reveals still guide believers, but the ceremonial laws (sacrifices, food laws, purity rules) and Israel’s civil laws pointed to Christ or applied to ancient Israel as a nation.

**Explanation:** Jesus said he came not to abolish the law but to fulfil it (Matthew 5:17). Hebrews explains that Christ is the final sacrifice. Mark 7:19 and Acts 10 show food laws no longer bind believers. The early church decided Gentile Christians did not need to follow the law of Moses (Acts 15). Christians fulfil the law’s heart by loving others (Romans 13:8–10).

**Key verses:** Matthew 5:17–20; Mark 7:18–19; Acts 10:9–16; Acts 15:1–29; Romans 10:4; Romans 13:8–10; Galatians 3:23–25; Hebrews 8:13

**Tags:** law, Old Testament, grace

### 14.16 Are tattoos a sin?

**Also asked:** Can Christians get tattoos?

**Short answer:** The Bible does not directly forbid modern tattoos for Christians. Leviticus 19:28 forbade Israel from marking the body for the dead, linked to pagan mourning practices. Today it is a matter of conscience, motives and wisdom.

**Explanation:** Christians should consider whether a tattoo honours God, what it communicates and whether it is done for right reasons (1 Corinthians 10:31; 6:19–20). Respect parents’ wishes when appropriate and avoid judging other believers on disputable matters (Romans 14).

**Key verses:** Leviticus 19:28; Romans 14:1–13; 1 Corinthians 6:19–20; 1 Corinthians 10:31

**Tags:** Christian liberty, ethics

### 14.17 How should Christians care for the poor and seek justice?

**Also asked:** Does God care about social justice?

**Short answer:** God cares deeply about the poor, the oppressed, widows, orphans and foreigners. Christians are called to act justly, show mercy, share generously and speak up for the vulnerable as an expression of their faith.

**Explanation:** The prophets repeatedly condemned injustice (Amos 5:24; Isaiah 1:17). Jesus identified with the hungry and needy (Matthew 25:35–40). James 1:27 says pure religion includes caring for orphans and widows. Good works do not save, but they flow from saving faith.

**Key verses:** Proverbs 31:8–9; Isaiah 1:17; Amos 5:24; Micah 6:8; Matthew 25:31–46; James 1:27; James 2:14–17

**Tags:** justice, poverty, mercy, ethics

### 14.18 Should Christians obey the government?

**Also asked:** Should Christians be involved in politics? · When can Christians disobey the law?

**Short answer:** Christians are called to respect and obey governing authorities, pay taxes and pray for leaders, because God established government for order and justice. But when the government commands what God forbids, or forbids what God commands, Christians must obey God.

**Explanation:** Romans 13:1–7 and 1 Peter 2:13–17 teach submission to authorities. When ordered to stop preaching about Jesus, the apostles replied, “We must obey God rather than men” (Acts 5:29). Christians may participate in public life in many ways, holding different political views, while their ultimate loyalty is to Christ’s kingdom.

**Key verses:** Jeremiah 29:7; Daniel 3; Acts 5:29; Romans 13:1–7; 1 Timothy 2:1–2; 1 Peter 2:13–17

**Tags:** government, citizenship, ethics

## 16. Bible Characters

Short profiles of the people the Bible’s story turns on, so the chatbot can answer “Who was …?” questions. Each one gives the person’s part in the story, what we can learn from them, and how their life points to Jesus.

### 16.1 Who are the key people in the Bible?

**Also asked:** bible characters · main characters in the Bible · important people in the Bible · bible heroes · who are the people in the Bible · famous people in the Bible

**Short answer:** The Bible tells one story through the lives of real people, from Adam and Eve at the beginning to the apostles of the early church. Knowing the main figures helps the whole story make sense, and each of them points in some way to Jesus.

**Explanation:** The first people are Adam and Eve, followed by Noah, who survived the flood. God then chose Abraham, whose family became Israel through Isaac, Jacob and Joseph. Moses led them out of Egypt and Joshua into the promised land, then judges such as Deborah ruled until the time of Ruth and the prophet Samuel. David became Israel’s great king, and his son Solomon built the temple. Prophets such as Elijah, Isaiah, Jeremiah and Daniel spoke for God, while Esther, Job and Jonah show God at work in ordinary lives, hardship and failure. In the New Testament the story turns on Jesus, with Mary his mother, John the Baptist who prepared the way, and the apostles Peter, John and Paul who carried the message across the world. Ask about any of them by name.

**Key verses:** Hebrews 11:1–40; Luke 24:27; Romans 15:4

**Tags:** bible characters, people in the bible, heroes of faith

### 16.2 Who were Adam and Eve?

**Also asked:** adam and eve · adam · eve · tell me about Adam and Eve · the first man and woman · garden of eden · who was the first person in the Bible

**Short answer:** Adam and Eve were the first man and woman, made by God in his image and placed in the garden of Eden. Their choice to disobey God let sin and death into the world, which is why the world is as broken as it is and why Jesus came.

**Explanation:** God formed Adam from the dust, breathed life into him and gave him Eve as his partner. They were to care for creation and walk with God (Genesis 1–2). A serpent tempted Eve to eat the fruit God had forbidden, and Adam ate with her (Genesis 3). Their relationship with God, with each other and with creation was damaged, and they were sent out of the garden, yet God promised that the offspring of the woman would one day defeat the serpent (Genesis 3:15). Christians see that promise fulfilled in Jesus. Paul calls Jesus the “last Adam”: as one man’s disobedience brought death to all, so one man’s obedience brings life (Romans 5:12–19; 1 Corinthians 15:22, 45). Believers hold differing views on how to read Genesis 1–3, covered under the questions on creation.

**Key verses:** Genesis 1:26–28; Genesis 2:7–25; Genesis 3:1–24; Romans 5:12–19; 1 Corinthians 15:21–22, 45

**Tags:** adam, eve, adam and eve, garden of eden, the fall, first humans, bible characters

### 16.3 Who was Noah?

**Also asked:** noah · tell me about Noah · noah’s ark · story of Noah · the flood · who built the ark

**Short answer:** Noah was a righteous man who obeyed God by building an ark when God sent a flood to judge a violent world. He and his family were saved, and God made a promise never again to destroy the earth with a flood.

**Explanation:** The Bible says people had become so wicked that God grieved over his creation, but “Noah found favour in the eyes of the Lord” (Genesis 6:5–8). God told him to build an ark, and Noah did everything God commanded. He took his family and the animals on board and God shut the door. After the waters went down, God made a covenant with Noah and set the rainbow in the sky as its sign (Genesis 9:8–17). The New Testament holds Noah up as a man of faith (Hebrews 11:7), and Peter compares the ark to salvation: Noah’s household was brought safely through the water, as believers are through Christ (1 Peter 3:20–21). Jesus also used the days of Noah to teach that his return will come when people are not ready (Matthew 24:37–39).

**Key verses:** Genesis 6:5–22; Genesis 7:1–5; Genesis 9:8–17; Hebrews 11:7; 1 Peter 3:20–21

**Tags:** noah, the ark, the flood, rainbow, covenant, bible characters

### 16.4 Who was Abraham?

**Also asked:** abraham · abram · tell me about Abraham · father of faith · story of Abraham · who is the father of the Jewish people

**Short answer:** Abraham was the man God called to leave his home and trust him, promising to make him a great nation and to bless all peoples through him. He is the father of Israel and, in the New Testament, the great example of being counted right with God by faith.

**Explanation:** God called Abram (later Abraham) from Ur, promising him land, a great family and blessing for the whole world (Genesis 12:1–3). He was old and childless, yet he believed God, and “it was counted to him as righteousness” (Genesis 15:6). God made a covenant with him, changed his name and, when he was a hundred, gave him a son, Isaac. Later God tested Abraham by asking for Isaac, and Abraham obeyed, trusting God to provide; God stopped him and provided a ram (Genesis 22). Abraham was not flawless: he lied about his wife Sarah and fathered Ishmael through Hagar when he doubted the promise would come. Paul teaches that Abraham was made right with God by faith, before the law, so that all who believe, Jew or not, are his spiritual children (Romans 4; Galatians 3:6–9). The “offspring” through whom the world would be blessed is Christ (Galatians 3:16).

**Key verses:** Genesis 12:1–3; Genesis 15:1–6; Genesis 17:1–8; Genesis 22:1–19; Romans 4:1–5, 16–22; Hebrews 11:8–19

**Tags:** abraham, abram, sarah, isaac, covenant, faith, bible characters

### 16.5 Who were Isaac and Jacob?

**Also asked:** isaac · jacob · israel · tell me about Isaac and Jacob · twelve tribes of Israel · why was Jacob renamed Israel · Jacob and Esau

**Short answer:** Isaac was Abraham’s promised son, and his son Jacob was renamed Israel. Jacob’s twelve sons became the twelve tribes of the nation of Israel, so these two carry God’s promise to Abraham forward.

**Explanation:** Isaac was born to Abraham and Sarah when they were old, and God confirmed the covenant promises to him (Genesis 21; 26). He married Rebekah, and their twin sons were Esau and Jacob. Jacob, whose name means “he grasps the heel” or “deceiver,” tricked his brother out of the birthright and blessing and had to flee (Genesis 25; 27). On the way, at Bethel, God appeared to him in a dream of a ladder to heaven and renewed the promise (Genesis 28:10–22). Years later Jacob wrestled with God through the night and was renamed Israel (Genesis 32:22–32). Of his twelve sons came the twelve tribes, among them Judah, from whom Jesus descended (Genesis 49:10; Matthew 1:2–3). Their story shows God keeping his promises through flawed people, by grace rather than by merit.

**Key verses:** Genesis 21:1–7; Genesis 25:19–34; Genesis 28:10–22; Genesis 32:22–32; Genesis 35:9–12; Romans 9:10–13

**Tags:** isaac, jacob, israel, esau, rebekah, twelve tribes, bible characters

### 16.6 Who was Joseph?

**Also asked:** joseph · joseph in egypt · tell me about Joseph · coat of many colours · story of Joseph · Joseph and his brothers

**Short answer:** Joseph was Jacob’s son, sold into slavery by his jealous brothers, who rose to rule Egypt under Pharaoh and saved many people from famine. He told his brothers that what they meant for evil, God meant for good.

**Explanation:** Jacob loved Joseph most and gave him a special robe; his brothers hated him and sold him to traders who took him to Egypt (Genesis 37). In Egypt he was falsely accused and imprisoned, yet “the Lord was with him” (Genesis 39). Interpreting Pharaoh’s dreams, he warned of seven years of plenty followed by seven of famine, and Pharaoh made him second in command (Genesis 41). When famine brought his brothers to Egypt, Joseph tested them, forgave them and moved the whole family there, which sets up Israel’s later time in Egypt (Genesis 42–47). After Jacob died he told his frightened brothers, “You meant evil against me, but God meant it for good” (Genesis 50:20). Many Christians see in Joseph’s story, of being rejected, suffering and then raised to save the very people who wronged him, a picture of Jesus.

**Key verses:** Genesis 37:3–28; Genesis 39:2–5, 20–23; Genesis 41:37–45; Genesis 45:4–8; Genesis 50:19–21; Romans 8:28

**Tags:** joseph, egypt, pharaoh, coat of many colours, forgiveness, bible characters

### 16.7 Who was Moses?

**Also asked:** moses · tell me about Moses · story of Moses · who led Israel out of Egypt · who gave the Ten Commandments · who parted the Red Sea

**Short answer:** Moses was the prophet God used to rescue Israel from slavery in Egypt, to give them his law at Mount Sinai, and to lead them for forty years towards the promised land. He is the key human figure of the Old Testament and is remembered as the one who spoke with God “face to face.”

**Explanation:** Born to Hebrew parents during a time when Pharaoh ordered Hebrew baby boys killed, Moses was hidden, found by Pharaoh’s daughter and raised in the palace (Exodus 2). At forty he fled Egypt after killing an Egyptian, and spent forty years as a shepherd. God called him from a burning bush, revealed his name (“I AM”) and sent him back to demand Israel’s freedom (Exodus 3–4). After the ten plagues, Israel left Egypt at the Passover, crossed the sea on dry ground and received the law, including the Ten Commandments, at Sinai (Exodus 12–20). Moses was not perfect: he struggled with self-doubt, anger and, at Meribah, disobedience, and he died on Mount Nebo seeing the promised land from a distance (Numbers 20:7–12; Deuteronomy 34). The New Testament sees him as a faithful servant who pointed beyond himself to Jesus, a greater rescuer and the one who brings a better covenant (Hebrews 3:1–6). At the transfiguration, Moses appeared with Jesus (Matthew 17:1–8).

**Key verses:** Exodus 3:1–15; Exodus 12:31–42; Exodus 14:21–31; Exodus 20:1–17; Numbers 12:3–8; Deuteronomy 34:10–12; Hebrews 3:1–6

**Tags:** moses, exodus, ten commandments, passover, red sea, sinai, bible characters

### 16.8 Who was Joshua?

**Also asked:** joshua · tell me about Joshua · joshua and the walls of Jericho · who led Israel into the promised land · battle of Jericho · be strong and courageous

**Short answer:** Joshua was Moses’ assistant and successor, who led Israel across the Jordan into the promised land. He is remembered for the fall of Jericho and for his call to serve the Lord faithfully.

**Explanation:** Joshua served Moses for decades and was one of two spies who trusted God’s promise to give Israel the land (Numbers 13–14). After Moses died, God told him to be “strong and courageous,” promising “I will be with you” (Joshua 1:1–9). Israel crossed the Jordan on dry ground, and the walls of Jericho fell after the people marched around the city in obedience to God’s instructions (Joshua 3; 6). Under Joshua, the tribes took possession of the land and it was divided among them. At the end of his life he challenged the nation, “Choose this day whom you will serve … as for me and my house, we will serve the Lord” (Joshua 24:15). His Hebrew name, Yehoshua, is the same as “Jesus” (“the Lord saves”), and many see Joshua leading God’s people to their rest as a pointer to Jesus leading his people into eternal rest (Hebrews 4:8–9).

**Key verses:** Joshua 1:1–9; Joshua 3:14–17; Joshua 6:1–20; Joshua 24:14–15; Hebrews 4:8–9

**Tags:** joshua, jericho, promised land, jordan, bible characters

### 16.9 Who was Deborah?

**Also asked:** deborah · tell me about Deborah · who was Deborah in the Bible · female judge · woman judge of Israel · Deborah and Barak

**Short answer:** Deborah was a prophet and judge who led Israel at a time of oppression. She called Barak to lead the army, trusted God for victory, and the nation enjoyed forty years of peace.

**Explanation:** In the days of the judges, Israel kept turning from God and was oppressed by the Canaanite king Jabin and his commander Sisera. Deborah, a prophet, judged Israel from under a palm tree and was known for her wisdom (Judges 4:4–5). She summoned Barak with God’s command to gather an army. Barak wanted her with him, so she went and told him the credit for the victory would go to a woman. God threw Sisera’s forces into panic, and Sisera was killed by Jael, the wife of Heber, in her tent (Judges 4–5). Deborah and Barak then sang the song recorded in Judges 5, praising God for the victory. Deborah is an example of a leader who listened to God, acted with courage and gave God the glory. Christians differ on how her example bears on leadership roles for women in the church, a question covered elsewhere.

**Key verses:** Judges 4:4–10; Judges 4:14–16; Judges 5:1–12

**Tags:** deborah, judges, barak, jael, sisera, bible characters

### 16.10 Who was Ruth?

**Also asked:** ruth · tell me about Ruth · book of Ruth · Ruth and Naomi · Ruth and Boaz · where you go I will go

**Short answer:** Ruth was a young widow from Moab who stayed loyal to her mother-in-law Naomi and came to trust Israel’s God. She married Boaz and became the great-grandmother of King David, and so an ancestor of Jesus.

**Explanation:** Naomi’s husband and sons died while the family lived in Moab, leaving her with her two daughters-in-law. When Naomi decided to return to Bethlehem, Ruth refused to leave her: “Where you go I will go … your people shall be my people, and your God my God” (Ruth 1:16–17). In Bethlehem, Ruth gathered leftover grain in the field of Boaz, a relative of Naomi’s late husband, who treated her with kindness and protected her. Boaz acted as their “kinsman-redeemer,” buying back the family land and marrying Ruth (Ruth 4). Their son Obed became the grandfather of David (Ruth 4:17–22). Ruth was a foreigner, yet God included her in the line of the Messiah (Matthew 1:5), a picture of how Christ welcomes people from every nation. Boaz’s redemption of Ruth is also seen as a picture of Jesus redeeming us.

**Key verses:** Ruth 1:16–17; Ruth 2:8–12; Ruth 4:9–17; Matthew 1:5

**Tags:** ruth, naomi, boaz, kinsman redeemer, moab, bible characters

### 16.11 Who was Samuel?

**Also asked:** samuel · tell me about Samuel · hannah and samuel · who anointed David · last judge of Israel · Samuel the prophet

**Short answer:** Samuel was the last of Israel’s judges and a prophet who anointed its first two kings, Saul and David. His mother Hannah had prayed for him and given him to serve God from childhood.

**Explanation:** Hannah, who was unable to have children, prayed for a son and promised to give him to the Lord. Samuel was raised in the sanctuary at Shiloh under the priest Eli, and as a boy he heard God call his name in the night, answering, “Speak, for your servant hears” (1 Samuel 1–3). As an adult he led Israel back to God and judged the nation faithfully (1 Samuel 7). When the people asked for a king to be like other nations, Samuel warned them, but at God’s direction he anointed Saul (1 Samuel 8–10). When Saul disobeyed, God rejected him as king and sent Samuel to Bethlehem to anoint David, a shepherd boy, because “the Lord looks on the heart” (1 Samuel 16:1–13). Samuel stands as a model of someone who listens to God and speaks for him without fear.

**Key verses:** 1 Samuel 1:9–20; 1 Samuel 3:1–10; 1 Samuel 8:4–9; 1 Samuel 16:1–13

**Tags:** samuel, hannah, eli, saul, prophet, bible characters

### 16.12 Who was David?

**Also asked:** david · king david · tell me about David · david and goliath · story of David · who killed Goliath · david and bathsheba

**Short answer:** David was a shepherd boy who became Israel’s greatest king, a man the Bible calls “after God’s own heart.” He wrote many of the Psalms, failed badly, repented, and God promised that the Messiah would come from his family.

**Explanation:** Anointed by Samuel while still a shepherd, David first became known by defeating the giant Goliath with a sling and a stone, trusting in the Lord rather than in armour (1 Samuel 16–17). For years he was hunted by the jealous King Saul but refused to harm him. As king he united the tribes, made Jerusalem his capital and wrote many of the Psalms, including Psalm 23. His greatest failure was committing adultery with Bathsheba and arranging the death of her husband Uriah; when the prophet Nathan confronted him he repented, and Psalm 51 records his prayer (2 Samuel 11–12). God made a lasting covenant with David, promising a descendant who would reign forever (2 Samuel 7:12–16). The New Testament names Jesus as the “Son of David,” the promised king (Matthew 1:1; Luke 1:32–33). David is a reminder that God uses flawed people and forgives those who truly repent.

**Key verses:** 1 Samuel 16:7, 13; 1 Samuel 17:45–50; 2 Samuel 7:12–16; 2 Samuel 12:1–13; Psalm 23; Psalm 51; Acts 13:22

**Tags:** david, goliath, bathsheba, psalms, king david, bible characters

### 16.13 Who was Solomon?

**Also asked:** solomon · king solomon · tell me about Solomon · wisest man in the Bible · who built the temple · solomon’s wisdom

**Short answer:** Solomon was David’s son and Israel’s third king, famous for his God-given wisdom and for building the temple in Jerusalem. His reign began in splendour, but his many foreign wives led his heart away from God.

**Explanation:** When God offered Solomon anything he wished for, he asked for an understanding mind to govern God’s people, and God gave him wisdom, riches and honour besides (1 Kings 3:5–14). He built the temple in Jerusalem, the place of God’s presence, and prayed a long prayer at its dedication (1 Kings 6–8). He is traditionally credited with much of Proverbs, as well as Ecclesiastes and the Song of Solomon. Solomon’s fame brought visitors from far away, including the queen of Sheba (1 Kings 10). But he married many foreign women who turned his heart to other gods, and after his death the kingdom split in two (1 Kings 11). His life warns that great gifts and success do not replace daily obedience. Jesus said that he himself is greater than Solomon (Matthew 12:42).

**Key verses:** 1 Kings 3:5–14; 1 Kings 8:22–30; 1 Kings 11:1–13; Proverbs 1:1–7; Matthew 12:42

**Tags:** solomon, king solomon, temple, wisdom, proverbs, bible characters

### 16.14 Who was Elijah?

**Also asked:** elijah · tell me about Elijah · elijah and the prophets of Baal · elijah on Mount Carmel · who went to heaven in a chariot of fire · still small voice

**Short answer:** Elijah was one of Israel’s greatest prophets, who stood against King Ahab and the worship of Baal. He is remembered for the showdown on Mount Carmel, and for being taken up to heaven in a whirlwind without dying.

**Explanation:** Elijah announced a drought to a nation that had turned to Baal, and God fed him by ravens and through a widow (1 Kings 17). On Mount Carmel he challenged 450 prophets of Baal to prove whose god was real. Baal did not answer, but fire fell from heaven for the Lord and the people declared, “The Lord, he is God” (1 Kings 18). Afterwards, afraid and exhausted, Elijah ran into the desert and asked to die; God cared for him and spoke not in wind or fire but in “a low whisper” (1 Kings 19:1–18). He passed his work to Elisha and was taken up in a whirlwind (2 Kings 2:11). Elijah appeared with Moses at the transfiguration (Matthew 17:1–8), and Jesus said that John the Baptist came in the spirit and power of Elijah (Luke 1:17; Matthew 11:14). The apostle James notes Elijah was “a man with a nature like ours,” and his prayers were effective (James 5:17–18).

**Key verses:** 1 Kings 17:1–6; 1 Kings 18:36–39; 1 Kings 19:9–13; 2 Kings 2:11; James 5:17–18

**Tags:** elijah, elisha, baal, mount carmel, prophet, bible characters

### 16.15 Who was Esther?

**Also asked:** esther · queen esther · tell me about Esther · book of Esther · purim · for such a time as this

**Short answer:** Esther was a Jewish woman who became queen of Persia and risked her life to save her people from a plot to destroy them. Her story shows God at work behind the scenes, even though his name is never mentioned in the book.

**Explanation:** After Queen Vashti was removed, the Persian king Ahasuerus chose Esther, an orphan raised by her cousin Mordecai, as his new queen. She kept her Jewish identity quiet (Esther 2). The king’s official Haman, angry that Mordecai refused to bow to him, tricked the king into ordering all Jews in the empire killed. Mordecai urged Esther to speak up, asking, “Who knows whether you have not come to the kingdom for such a time as this?” (Esther 4:14). She fasted, then went to the king unasked and revealed Haman’s plot. Haman was punished and the Jews were allowed to defend themselves (Esther 7–9). The Jewish festival of Purim celebrates this deliverance. The book is a picture of God’s care and timing, working through ordinary people and events to keep his promises.

**Key verses:** Esther 2:17; Esther 4:13–16; Esther 7:1–6; Esther 9:20–22

**Tags:** esther, mordecai, haman, purim, persia, bible characters

### 16.16 Who was Job?

**Also asked:** job · tell me about Job · book of Job · story of Job · patience of Job · why did Job suffer

**Short answer:** Job was a good and faithful man who lost his children, wealth and health in a single season of terrible suffering. The book of Job does not give a neat explanation, but shows that God is wise, present and worthy of trust in the dark.

**Explanation:** The book opens with Job “blameless and upright,” then records the loss of his children and possessions in a single day, followed by painful sores (Job 1–2). Job’s wife and three friends come to him; the friends insist his suffering must be punishment for hidden sin, which the book shows to be wrong. Job laments honestly and asks God for an answer, yet keeps his faith, declaring, “I know that my Redeemer lives” (Job 19:25). In the end God speaks out of the whirlwind, not explaining the reasons but pointing Job to his power and wisdom as creator (Job 38–41). Job humbly trusts him, and God restores him and rebukes the friends (Job 42). James holds up Job as an example of perseverance, and notes the Lord is full of compassion and mercy (James 5:11).

**Key verses:** Job 1:20–22; Job 19:25–27; Job 38:1–7; Job 42:1–6; James 5:11

**Tags:** job, suffering, patience, redeemer, bible characters

### 16.17 Who was Isaiah?

**Also asked:** isaiah · the prophet isaiah · tell me about Isaiah · book of Isaiah · who prophesied about Jesus · isaiah 53

**Short answer:** Isaiah was a prophet in Jerusalem about 700 years before Christ who warned of judgment and spoke of comfort and hope. His book contains some of the clearest promises of the coming Messiah, including the suffering servant of Isaiah 53.

**Explanation:** Isaiah was called in a vision of the Lord high and lifted up, and answered, “Here I am! Send me” (Isaiah 6:1–8). He prophesied in Judah during the reigns of several kings, calling the nation to turn back to God and warning of exile. His book also looks forward to a child called Immanuel, “God with us” (Isaiah 7:14), a son given whose names include “Mighty God” and “Prince of Peace” (Isaiah 9:6–7), and a servant who “was pierced for our transgressions” and bears the sins of many (Isaiah 53). Chapters 40 to 66 offer comfort and the promise of a new heaven and earth. The New Testament quotes Isaiah more than any other prophet, and the early Christians saw Jesus as the fulfilment of these promises (Matthew 1:22–23; Acts 8:26–35).

**Key verses:** Isaiah 6:1–8; Isaiah 7:14; Isaiah 9:6–7; Isaiah 40:28–31; Isaiah 53:1–6; Acts 8:30–35

**Tags:** isaiah, prophet, messiah, suffering servant, immanuel, bible characters

### 16.18 Who was Jeremiah?

**Also asked:** jeremiah · the prophet jeremiah · tell me about Jeremiah · weeping prophet · jeremiah 29:11 · new covenant jeremiah

**Short answer:** Jeremiah was a prophet who warned Jerusalem of judgment for forty years and was mocked and persecuted for it. He also announced God’s promise of a new covenant and a hopeful future.

**Explanation:** God called Jeremiah as a young man, and though he said “I am only a youth,” God promised, “I am with you to deliver you” (Jeremiah 1:4–10). He preached to a nation that ignored God and refused to listen, was beaten, put in stocks and thrown into a cistern, and lived to see Babylon destroy Jerusalem in 586 BC (Jeremiah 20; 38; 39). He is called the “weeping prophet” for his grief over the people. Yet Jeremiah also promised restoration. His letter to the exiles in Babylon includes the well-known words, “I know the plans I have for you … plans for welfare and not for evil, to give you a future and a hope” (Jeremiah 29:11), a promise to the exiles as a people. Most importantly, he foretold a new covenant, with God’s law written on people’s hearts and sins forgiven (Jeremiah 31:31–34), which Jesus fulfilled at the Last Supper and through his death (Luke 22:20).

**Key verses:** Jeremiah 1:4–10; Jeremiah 20:7–9; Jeremiah 29:4–14; Jeremiah 31:31–34; Luke 22:20

**Tags:** jeremiah, prophet, exile, new covenant, bible characters

### 16.19 Who was Daniel?

**Also asked:** daniel · tell me about Daniel · story of Daniel · Daniel in the lions’ den · who was Daniel in the Bible · what happened to Daniel in Babylon

**Short answer:** Daniel was a young Jewish man taken to Babylon, who rose to high office there while staying faithful to God. He is remembered for the lions’ den, for interpreting dreams, and for visions that look ahead to God’s everlasting kingdom.

**Explanation:** Daniel was among the Israelites carried off to Babylon around 605 BC. With his friends Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego he refused to defile himself with the king’s food, and God gave him wisdom (Daniel 1). He interpreted King Nebuchadnezzar’s dream and was honoured for it (Daniel 2). His three friends were later thrown into a blazing furnace for refusing to worship a golden statue, and God preserved them (Daniel 3). Years later, under the Persian king Darius, Daniel kept praying three times a day despite a law forbidding it. He was thrown to the lions and God shut their mouths (Daniel 6). The second half of the book records Daniel’s visions of world kingdoms and of “one like a son of man” who receives an eternal kingdom (Daniel 7:13–14), a picture Jesus later applied to himself. Daniel shows how to live faithfully as a believer in a culture that does not share your faith.

**Key verses:** Daniel 1:8–17; Daniel 3:16–18; Daniel 6:10–23; Daniel 7:13–14; Daniel 9:3–19; Daniel 12:2–3

**Tags:** daniel, babylon, lions den, shadrach meshach and abednego, exile, bible characters

### 16.20 Who was Jonah?

**Also asked:** jonah · tell me about Jonah · jonah and the whale · jonah and the big fish · story of Jonah · why did Jonah run from God · nineveh

**Short answer:** Jonah was a prophet God sent to warn the city of Nineveh, who ran the other way and was swallowed by a great fish. The book shows God’s mercy reaching even a hated enemy, and how reluctant his own prophet was to share it.

**Explanation:** God told Jonah to go to Nineveh, the capital of Assyria, Israel’s cruel enemy. Jonah boarded a ship for the opposite direction, a storm struck, and he was thrown overboard and swallowed by a great fish, where he prayed for three days before being brought back to land (Jonah 1–2). He then obeyed and preached, and the whole city, king included, repented and God spared them (Jonah 3). Jonah was angry about this because he knew God is “gracious and merciful, slow to anger” (Jonah 4:2). The book ends with God asking Jonah whether he should not care about that great city (Jonah 4:11). Jesus pointed to Jonah’s three days in the fish as a sign of his own death and resurrection, and said Nineveh’s repentance would condemn those who refused to listen to someone greater than Jonah (Matthew 12:39–41).

**Key verses:** Jonah 1:1–3; Jonah 2:1–2, 10; Jonah 3:5–10; Jonah 4:1–2, 10–11; Matthew 12:39–41

**Tags:** jonah, nineveh, big fish, whale, prophet, mercy, bible characters

### 16.21 Who was Mary, the mother of Jesus?

**Also asked:** mary · mary mother of jesus · tell me about Mary · virgin mary · who was Jesus’ mother · mary and joseph

**Short answer:** Mary was the young woman God chose to give birth to Jesus by the power of the Holy Spirit. She is honoured in the Bible as a humble servant of God who trusted his word, and as the mother who watched her Son die and later joined the first believers.

**Explanation:** An angel told Mary, a young woman engaged to Joseph, that she would conceive a son and name him Jesus. She replied, “Behold, I am the servant of the Lord; let it be to me according to your word” (Luke 1:26–38). Her song of praise, the Magnificat, celebrates God lifting up the lowly (Luke 1:46–55). She gave birth in Bethlehem, was present at Jesus’ first miracle at Cana, and stood by the cross, where Jesus entrusted her to the apostle John (Luke 2; John 2:1–11; John 19:25–27). After the resurrection she is among the believers praying in Jerusalem (Acts 1:14). Christians of all traditions honour Mary. Protestants and evangelicals hold that, though blessed, she was a sinner who needed a Saviour like everyone else (Luke 1:47), and that prayer and worship belong to God alone. Catholic and Orthodox Christians give her a larger place in devotion.

**Key verses:** Luke 1:26–38; Luke 1:46–55; Luke 2:1–7; John 2:1–5; John 19:25–27; Acts 1:14

**Tags:** mary, mother of jesus, virgin birth, magnificat, bible characters

### 16.22 Who was John the Baptist?

**Also asked:** john the baptist · baptist · tell me about John the Baptist · who baptised Jesus · forerunner of Jesus · voice in the wilderness

**Short answer:** John the Baptist was the prophet who prepared the way for Jesus by calling people to repent and be baptised. He pointed to Jesus as “the Lamb of God, who takes away the sin of the world,” and he baptised Jesus in the Jordan.

**Explanation:** John was born to the priest Zechariah and Elizabeth, a relative of Mary, after an angel promised their long-awaited son (Luke 1). He lived in the wilderness, wore camel hair and preached repentance, fulfilling Isaiah’s words about a “voice crying in the wilderness: prepare the way of the Lord” (Matthew 3:1–3). Crowds came to be baptised in the Jordan, and John told them to produce fruit that shows a changed heart. When Jesus came, John baptised him and heard the Father’s voice from heaven (Matthew 3:13–17). He told his followers, “He must increase, but I must decrease” (John 3:30). John later challenged King Herod over his unlawful marriage, was imprisoned and was beheaded (Matthew 14:1–12). Jesus said that no one born of women was greater than John (Matthew 11:11).

**Key verses:** Luke 1:13–17; Matthew 3:1–17; John 1:29–34; John 3:27–30; Matthew 11:7–11

**Tags:** john the baptist, baptism, forerunner, repentance, bible characters

### 16.23 Who was Peter?

**Also asked:** peter · simon peter · apostle peter · tell me about Peter · peter who denied jesus · rock of the church · who walked on water

**Short answer:** Peter was a fisherman who became the leader among Jesus’ twelve apostles and a leading voice in the early church. He is remembered for his bold faith, for denying Jesus three times, and for being restored and used by God to preach at Pentecost.

**Explanation:** Jesus called Simon, a fisherman, saying, “Follow me, and I will make you fishers of men” and gave him the name Peter, “rock” (Matthew 4:18–20; John 1:42). Peter declared, “You are the Christ, the Son of the living God” (Matthew 16:13–20). He walked on water towards Jesus until fear made him sink (Matthew 14:28–31). On the night Jesus was arrested he denied knowing him three times and wept bitterly (Luke 22:54–62). After the resurrection Jesus restored him, asking three times “Do you love me?” and told him to feed his sheep (John 21:15–19). At Pentecost, Peter preached and about three thousand people believed (Acts 2). He later took the gospel to non-Jews (Acts 10) and wrote two New Testament letters. Christians differ on what Jesus meant by building his church “on this rock,” but all agree Peter was a key apostle and an example of grace.

**Key verses:** Matthew 4:18–20; Matthew 16:13–19; Luke 22:54–62; John 21:15–19; Acts 2:14–41; Acts 10:34–43

**Tags:** peter, simon peter, apostle, disciples, pentecost, denial, bible characters

### 16.24 Who was Paul?

**Also asked:** paul · saul · apostle paul · tell me about Paul · saul of tarsus · road to damascus · who wrote most of the New Testament

**Short answer:** Paul was a Pharisee who persecuted the church until the risen Jesus met him on the road to Damascus. He became the great apostle to the non-Jewish world, planted churches across the Roman Empire and wrote many of the New Testament letters.

**Explanation:** Born Saul in Tarsus, he was a strict, well-educated Pharisee who approved of Stephen’s killing and tried to destroy the church (Acts 7:58; 8:1–3). On the road to Damascus, Jesus appeared to him in blinding light and asked, “Why are you persecuting me?” He was baptised, and soon preached that Jesus is the Son of God (Acts 9:1–22). Over three missionary journeys he took the gospel to Asia Minor, Greece and Rome, planting churches, enduring beatings, shipwreck, prison and hardship (Acts 13–28; 2 Corinthians 11:23–28). Thirteen New Testament letters carry his name, teaching that people are saved by grace through faith, not by works of the law (Ephesians 2:8–9; Romans 3:21–26). Near the end, from prison in Rome, he wrote that he had “fought the good fight” and finished the race (2 Timothy 4:6–8). Tradition says he was executed in Rome.

**Key verses:** Acts 9:1–19; Philippians 3:4–11; Galatians 2:20; Ephesians 2:8–9; 2 Timothy 4:6–8

**Tags:** paul, saul, apostle paul, damascus road, missionary, epistles, bible characters

### 16.25 Who was John the apostle?

**Also asked:** john · apostle john · john the apostle · tell me about John · disciple whom Jesus loved · who wrote the gospel of John · who wrote Revelation

**Short answer:** John was one of Jesus’ closest disciples, a fisherman who followed him from the start and was at the cross. Christian tradition holds that he wrote the Gospel of John, three letters and the book of Revelation.

**Explanation:** John and his brother James were fishermen who left their boat and their father to follow Jesus (Matthew 4:21–22). With Peter and James, he was with Jesus at the transfiguration and in Gethsemane. Jesus nicknamed the brothers “sons of thunder” a name that fits their zeal (Mark 3:17; Luke 9:54). John is traditionally identified as “the disciple whom Jesus loved,” who leaned against Jesus at the Last Supper and is the only one of the Twelve recorded at the cross, where Jesus entrusted his mother Mary to him (John 13:23; 19:25–27). The Gospel of John was written “that you may believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God” (John 20:31). His letters stress that God is love and that believers must love one another (1 John 4:7–12). Revelation was written while John was exiled on the island of Patmos (Revelation 1:9). He is thought to have been the last of the Twelve to die.

**Key verses:** Mark 1:19–20; John 13:23; John 19:25–27; John 20:30–31; 1 John 4:7–12; Revelation 1:9

**Tags:** john, apostle john, disciple, gospel of john, revelation, patmos, bible characters
